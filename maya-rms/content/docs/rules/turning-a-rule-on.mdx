<InPlainWords>
Whenever a rule is about to start acting, a window opens first. It shows a small calendar of the months ahead with the days the rule will change filled in, and a line such as "41 days will be affected by this rule." Click <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> and the rule changes those days from the next pricing run. Click <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> and the rule is on, but those days keep their prices until the rule stops being true on a day and then becomes true again. Every other day works as if you had applied it. When nothing would change, the window says "0 prices will be affected by this rule." and <Ui>Turn it on</Ui> switches it on (<Ui>Save changes</Ui> saves an edit to a rule that is on). <Ui>Cancel</Ui> saves nothing.
</InPlainWords>

## When it opens

| What you do | The window |
|---|---|
| Switch a rule <Ui>On</Ui> in the rules list | Opens, headed "Turn on "Busy nights"?" |
| Click <Ui>Add Rule</Ui> in the rule builder | Opens, headed "Add "Busy nights"?" (a new rule is saved on) |
| Click <Ui>Save changes</Ui> on a rule that is on | Opens, headed "Save changes to "Busy nights"?", unless only the name changed |
| Click <Ui>Add this rule</Ui> or <Ui>Make that change</Ui> on a card from <Ui>Get suggestions from my data</Ui> | Opens |
| Switch a rule <Ui>Off</Ui> | Does not open: switching off is at once, and the rule's changes stay |
| Save a new name alone, or save changes to a rule that is off | Does not open: neither moves a price. An edited rule that is off shows the window when you switch it on |
| Accept a rule card in your first review, or the starter rules | Does not open: the property is being set up |
| <Ui>Save This Rule</Ui> in the <Ui>Rate Simulator</Ui> | Does not open: the rule arrives switched off. Switching it on opens the window |

## The calendar

The calendar starts at your property's today and runs to the last night MAYA prices, 395 nights on, a small block per month. That is 13 or 14 months, the first and last blocks usually partly empty, so every day the count includes can be seen. The first block and each January name their year. Each block has a row per week, Sunday first, as on the <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab, and one small square per night.

- A filled blue square is a day the rule will change.
- A dark square is a day it will not.
- Today has a thin outline.
- Days before today and after the last priced night are left blank.

Hover a blue square to read the day and how many room types change, such as "Sat 3 Oct 2026: prices change on 2 room types". A screen reader reads the affected days out as runs of dates.

## What the days mean

Under the calendar is one line: "41 days will be affected by this rule.", "1 day will be affected by this rule.", or "0 prices will be affected by this rule." A "?" beside it explains the rest.

A day counts when the rule changes the price of at least one room type that night. The days come from a trial run of the same pricing that runs every few minutes, done twice on your bookings as they are right now: once with the rule as you are about to save it, and once as things stand. The two sets of prices are compared night by night, to the cent. So everything else is already taken into account:

- every other rule, including a stronger rule that already covers the night, a rule waiting after its last change, and the one change a booking speed or pickup rule can make on a night per run
- prices you typed, comp nights at 0, and nights left unpriced
- your floors and ceilings: a raise on a night already at its ceiling moves nothing, so that day does not count

For a change to a rule that is on, "as things stand" is the rule as it is saved now. Left alone, it would act on what it matches today too, so a day also counts when the saved rule would change it and the edited one will not.

Nothing is saved while the window is open, and the days are the ones the next pricing run would change if nothing else changes first.

**0 prices.** "0 prices will be affected by this rule." means no price moves now: the rule is not true anywhere yet, or where it is, something else holds the price. With nothing to change, applying and skipping come to the same thing, so the window offers one button, <Ui>Turn it on</Ui>, and <Ui>Cancel</Ui>. For an edit to a rule that is already on, the button reads <Ui>Save changes</Ui> instead. Either one saves the rule on, as <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> does. From then on it acts on what becomes true, and a booking speed or pickup rule counts every booking on the books, the ones made before the rule included. If the days changed while the window was open, the new days show with <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> and <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> instead (read [When something changes while it is open](#when-something-changes-while-it-is-open)).

**A cut on low pickup with no booking window.** When the rule is <Ui>Decrease the rate</Ui> with a <Ui>Pickup count (advanced)</Ui> row set to <Ui>Less than</Ui> and no <Ui>Booking window (days to stay)</Ui> row, two amber lines follow the count, worked out in the same trial run: "With no booking window condition, this rule reaches 396 of the 396 nights ahead. A night that gained under 1 room night over the 7 full days before counts as quiet, and a far-out night with no bookings yet always will, once your rules have run for 1 week." and "The cut repeats: each time its wait of 1 week is over and the night is still quiet, it cuts again, on top of the cut before." The first number is every night the rule can act on (fewer when it has a date range or weekdays), the second is the window your rules price, and the amount, lookback and wait are the rule's own. The count above can be "0 prices" while your rules have run for less than the lookback window, and these lines say what happens once they have. The rule builder fills a booking window row in for you when you build a rule of this shape. Read [A cut on low pickup, far out](/docs/rules/pickup-count#a-cut-on-low-pickup-far-out).

## Apply price adjustments

The rule is saved and on. The days in blue are priced first on the next pricing run, about five minutes away, and the rest of the window follows over the next runs. The prices that result are the ones the calendar showed. The <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab and the change log show them as they land. In simulation nothing is sent. Live on Cloudbeds or ThinkReservations, the new prices are sent to your property system. On Mews, they change in MAYA only.

After an edit, Apply judges every change the rule already has on the price against the edited rule:

- a standard rule's change stays where the edited rule holds, at the new amount, and comes off where it does not, including on room types taken off the rule and nights the rule no longer covers
- a booking speed or pickup rule's earlier changes come off (the change log says "the rule was edited, so MAYA started it fresh"), and the edited rule reads its whole window again with no wait, so it can change a night again on the same run

Read [When a price change comes off](/docs/rules/when-a-change-comes-off#a-rule-that-is-changed).

## Skip price adjustments

The rule is saved and on, and the days in blue keep their prices. On each of those days the rule leaves its part of the price as it is until the rule stops being true there and then becomes true again. From then on it acts on that day as on any other. Every day that is not blue works exactly as if you had clicked <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui>.

- **A standard rule** (occupancy or booking window only): on a blue day where it was about to raise or cut, it is on with no change on the price. Once it stops being true there and later is true again, it adjusts.
- **A booking speed or pickup rule**: on a blue day it makes no change while it stays true. The first time it is true there after not being true, it adjusts, in that same run.

Whether the rule is true on a blue day is read from the night as it is now, whatever its undo box says. The box keeps a change the rule made through cancellations, and on a blue day the rule has made none, so with the box unticked too, cancellations that take a blue day under the rule's occupancy line mean the rule has stopped being true there.

**Counting.** Skip never changes where a rule counts from. A booking speed or pickup rule counts every booking on the books, the ones made before you added it included, whether you applied or skipped. So on a day that was not blue, a booking that arrives after you skip can make the rule adjust on the next run, counted together with the bookings before it.

**Changes already on the price.** Switching a rule that has changes on the price back on, or saving an edit to a rule that is on, leaves its changes on the blue days where they are:

- a standard rule's change the edit would have moved to the new amount stays at its old amount while the rule is true there, and still stays once it stops being true. The next time the rule is true there, it moves to the rule's amount.
- a standard rule's change the rule would have taken off stays at its amount. Where the rule is not true on that day, it stays until the rule is true there, then moves to the rule's amount, or comes off on a room type or night the rule no longer covers. Where the rule is true there but no longer covers that room type or night, it stays until the rule has stopped being true there and is true again, then comes off.
- a booking speed or pickup rule's changes stay, are not checked for cancellations while the day is held, and still count as the newest change on the night where the weaker rules count from. They rank as the rule did when it made them, so an edit that lowers the amount or changes the speed does not change which rules they cover. When the day's hold ends, the changes from before an edit come off and the edited rule adjusts in that same run.

They also come off when the night passes, when you type a price on the night, or when you delete the rule. On days that are not blue, the rule's changes are judged as Apply judges them (read [Apply price adjustments](#apply-price-adjustments)).

**Off and on again.** Switching the rule off freezes it, held days included. Switching it back on opens the window again. With <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> the held days end: every change the rule has on the price is judged as Apply judges it, so a change still at its old amount moves to the rule's amount, and the window counts those days. With <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> the days the window shows then are held instead. An edit saved with either choice does the same.

**After a Skip.** Skip itself never changes a price. The prices on the blue days move later only once the rule has stopped and started being true there, or when something else moves them: a price you type, or another rule.

<Example title="Skipping at The Harbour Inn">
Priya adds "Busy nights": more than 80% full, raise 10%. The window shows "6 days will be affected by this rule." She clicks <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui>. Friday, 17 of 20 booked, stays at $150 while it stays above 80%. On Saturday, 14 of 20 when she skipped, two bookings arrive and it reaches 16 of 20: Saturday was not blue, so Saturday goes to $165 on the next run. If Friday drops to 15 of 20 and later fills again, Friday gets the raise then.

She also adds "Weekend rush": booking speed at least Faster Than Normal over the past week, raise 8%, and skips again. Six bookings for Saturday 21 March came in last week, before the rule existed, and Saturday was not blue. One more arrives today: with all seven counted, Saturday reads Faster Than Normal, and it goes up 8% on the next run.
</Example>

## Cancel

<Ui>Cancel</Ui>, the Esc key or a click outside the window saves nothing. A rule you were switching on stays <Ui>Off</Ui>, and the rule builder keeps everything you typed, so you can change it and try again.

## When something changes while it is open

When you click <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui>, <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui>, <Ui>Turn it on</Ui> or <Ui>Save changes</Ui>, the save first checks whether anything that could move the days changed since they were worked out: a booking or a cancellation, a price typed, a pricing run, another rule, a room type. If something did, the days are worked out again. When they are the same, the rule is saved straight away, and a Skip holds those days. When they are not, nothing is saved, the calendar and the count show the new days, and a line reads "Your bookings changed while this was open, so the days were checked again." Choose again.

## When the days cannot be checked

If the check fails or takes too long, the line reads "We weren't able to calculate how many days would be affected by this rule." The window then offers three buttons, and no <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui>, since there is no count to apply:

- <Ui>Try again</Ui> works the days out again.
- <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> turns the rule on without changing any price now. With no days to go on, it holds every day the rule could change: each night in its scope and each night with a change of it on the price, each until the rule stops being true there and then becomes true again, as above.
- <Ui>Cancel</Ui> saves nothing.

When the check was refused because of too many checks at once, "That's a lot of checks at once. Give it a minute and try again." shows under that line.

## How long it takes

While the days are worked out, the squares are grey and pulse, the line reads "Checking your calendar…", and the buttons wait. A rule on occupancy or booking window is usually answered within a second or two. A booking speed or pickup rule takes longer, because every night it could act on is priced: its calendar is asked for in three parts at once, the nearest two months first, and the count appears only when all three are in, usually within a few seconds.

## Who can do this

Revenue Managers, General Managers and Hotel Admins. For a Viewer the window opens for a moment and closes again, and "Only a Revenue Manager or above can change this." appears under the rule's switch, or under the rule builder. Nothing is saved. Read [Roles: who can do what](/docs/team/roles).
