<InPlainWords>
A rule has a name, one to four conditions that must all be true, a direction and an amount, and the room types it changes. The four conditions are sellable occupancy, booking speed, booking window and pickup count, one of each at most. The amount is a percent or a fixed amount, one or the other. A box under the amount, ticked to start, lets cancellations undo the rule's changes. Click <Ui>Add Rule</Ui>, and a window shows the days the rule will change: apply or skip the price adjustments, and the rule is on. <Ui>Edit</Ui> on a saved rule opens it here to change.
</InPlainWords>

## Where the builder is

On the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab, click <Ui>+ Add a rule</Ui> under the list of rules. The form opens below the button and stays open until you click the button again. Everything you type stays in the form, even if you close it or switch to another tab and back, until the rule is saved or you reload the page. The room type chips go back to their starting selection when you switch property, or when you change <Ui>Counts as a room</Ui> or rooms out of service on the <Ui>PMS</Ui> tab.

On a Cloudbeds property, <Ui>Import from PIE</Ui> beside the <Ui>Pricing Rules</Ui> heading turns a screenshot of your PIE rules into MAYA rules instead. Its <Ui>Edit</Ui> opens one of them in this form before it is added, headed "Change before importing": the button at the bottom reads <Ui>Use in the import</Ui>, which takes the rule back to the import as changed without saving it, with <Ui>Back to the import</Ui> beside it to leave it as it was. Read [Import your rules from PIE](/docs/rules/import-your-rules-from-pie).

<Ui>Edit</Ui> on a rule's row opens the same form filled with that rule as saved, headed "Edit "Busy nights"" in place of <Ui>+ Add a rule</Ui>. Every field below is filled in, the undo box included, and the button at the bottom reads <Ui>Save changes</Ui>, with <Ui>Cancel editing</Ui> beside it to empty the form without saving. Switching property or deleting the rule also ends the edit. Read [Changing a rule](/docs/rules/off-delete-or-change#changing-a-rule).

## Rule name

The <Ui>Rule name</Ui> box is required. The placeholder reads "e.g. Weekend surge". There is no length limit. The name appears in the rules list, in every change log sentence the rule writes, and in the banner MAYA shows when a rule keeps adjusting a night, so choose something you will recognise later.

## Conditions

The <Ui to="rules.new" q="focus=conditions">Conditions</Ui> section is headed "Matches when all of the following are true." That sentence is the whole logic: a rule with two conditions acts only on nights where both hold. There is no either/or. If you want "busy or last-minute", make two rules.

A rule needs at least one condition and can have four at most, one of each kind. The first row is there when the form opens, set to occupancy. Click <Ui>+ Add condition</Ui> to add another row. Each new row starts on the next kind you have not used yet, in this order: occupancy, booking speed, booking window, pickup. Once a kind is used, it is greyed out in the other rows' <Ui>Metric</Ui> dropdown. When all four are in use, the button disappears.

Each row has a round red button labelled "Remove condition". Removing the last row puts a fresh occupancy row in its place, because a rule cannot be saved with no condition. Changing a row's <Ui>Metric</Ui> puts its <Ui>Threshold</Ui>, <Ui>Speed</Ui>, <Ui>Measured over</Ui>, <Ui>Lookback window</Ui> and <Ui>Pickup measures</Ui> back to their defaults. <Ui>Compare</Ui> and <Ui>Then waits (advanced)</Ui> keep whatever you had chosen, so check them after a switch.

A cut on low pickup with no booking window row gets one filled in for you. The moment your rule is <Ui>Decrease the rate</Ui> with a <Ui>Pickup count (advanced)</Ui> row set to <Ui>Less than</Ui> and no <Ui>Booking window (days to stay)</Ui> row, a booking window row appears, <Ui>Less than</Ui> 60, with a "?" beside its <Ui>Metric</Ui> label that says why: without it the rule reaches every night of the 396-night window, a far-out night with no bookings yet always counts as quiet, and the cut repeats each time the rule's wait is over. Change the number, or remove the row with its red button. It is offered once per rule you build or edit, never comes back on its own, and saving never adds it: the rule saved is exactly the rows on screen. Rules you saved before are left as they are, and <Ui>Edit</Ui> on one offers the row the same way. Read [A cut on low pickup, far out](/docs/rules/pickup-count#a-cut-on-low-pickup-far-out).

The <Ui>Metric</Ui> dropdown has four choices. Each has its own page.

| Metric label | What it measures | Fields on the row | Read more |
|---|---|---|---|
| <Ui>Sellable occupancy (%)</Ui> | Rooms booked divided by rooms you can sell that night | <Ui>Compare</Ui> (<Ui>Greater than</Ui> or <Ui>Less than</Ui>), <Ui>Threshold</Ui>, default Greater than 80 | [Sellable occupancy](/docs/rules/sellable-occupancy) |
| <Ui>Booking speed (recommended)</Ui> | How many bookings the night received recently, compared with similar past nights at the same point | <Ui>Speed</Ui> (seven levels, default <Ui>Faster Than Normal</Ui>), <Ui>Measured over</Ui> (default <Ui>Past week</Ui>), <Ui>Then waits (advanced)</Ui> (default <Ui>1 week</Ui>) | [Booking speed](/docs/rules/booking-speed) |
| <Ui>Booking window (days to stay)</Ui> | Days until the stay, on your property's own calendar (today is 0) | <Ui>Compare</Ui>, <Ui>Threshold</Ui>, default Greater than 7 | [Booking window](/docs/rules/booking-window) |
| <Ui>Pickup count (advanced)</Ui> | Room nights (or revenue) the night gained in the last 1, 3 or 7 days | <Ui>Compare</Ui>, <Ui>Threshold</Ui> (default Greater than 5), <Ui>Lookback window</Ui> (default <Ui>3 days</Ui>), <Ui>Then waits (advanced)</Ui> (default <Ui>Same as the lookback window</Ui>), <Ui>Pickup measures</Ui> (default <Ui>Room nights (units)</Ui>) | [Pickup count (advanced)](/docs/rules/pickup-count) |

A few things about the number fields:

- <Ui>Threshold</Ui> must be 0 or more. If it is blank or below 0, your browser stops the save and points at the box. A typed 0 is accepted.
- Comparisons are strict. <Ui>Greater than</Ui> 80 means more than 80, not 80 exactly. <Ui>Less than</Ui> 7 means 6 or fewer.
- An occupancy threshold above 100 is saved as 100. A booking window threshold is rounded to whole days.
- Booking speed has no <Ui>Compare</Ui> field. Picking a level above Normal means "that fast or faster", a level below Normal means "that slow or slower", and <Ui>Normal</Ui> means exactly Normal.

<Callout kind="good-to-know">
Beside <Ui>Measured over</Ui> and each <Ui>Then waits (advanced)</Ui> there is a "?" you can hover or focus. It opens a short panel that says how booking speed is counted or what the wait does, what the rule counts once a night has changed, and what makes one rule stronger than another. The same is on the [Booking speed](/docs/rules/booking-speed) and [Pickup count](/docs/rules/pickup-count) pages.
</Callout>

## Rate adjustment

The <Ui>Rate adjustment</Ui> section has a dropdown and two boxes.

<Ui>Direction (required)</Ui> starts blank on purpose, showing "Choose: increase or decrease the rate…". Pick <Ui>Increase the rate</Ui> or <Ui>Decrease the rate</Ui>. If you leave it blank, your browser stops the save and points at the dropdown.

<Ui>Adjust by percent (%)</Ui> is a number box, empty to start, showing "e.g. 10" until you type. A percent change multiplies the price: 10% on $150 gives $165, and when two percent rules land on the same night they compound (10% then 25% on $200 gives $275, not $270).

<Ui>Adjust by fixed amount ($)</Ui> is the number box under it, also empty, showing "e.g. 15". A fixed amount adds to or subtracts from the price: $25 on $150 gives $175. The box shows a $ sign whatever your property's currency. The amount is in your property's currency.

A rule has one amount. Type a number in one box and the other is greyed out until you clear it. Whichever box has the number is the amount the rule saves. For a raise of 10% plus $5, make two rules: they stack.

Type the amount as a positive number. The direction dropdown supplies the sign. If you type a negative number, your browser stops the save and points at the box.

Under the amounts is one more checkbox, <Ui>Undo the change if cancellations mean this rule is no longer true</Ui>, ticked to start. Ticked, a change the rule makes comes off when guests cancel and the rule is no longer true for that night. Unticked, cancellations never take its changes off. The rules list does not show it. To change it on a saved rule, click <Ui>Edit</Ui> on the rule. Read [The undo box](/docs/rules/when-a-change-comes-off#the-undo-box).

**"Is there a limit on how big a raise or cut can be?"** Not one you would meet: any amount above 0 saves in either box, up to just under 1,000,000. A raise stops at the room type's ceiling. A cut that would take the price below the floor publishes the floor instead. A cut of 100% or more, or a fixed cut as big as the price, takes the price to 0 or below on its own, so the room type's floor is published, and the floor is 1.00 in your property's currency until you set one. A typo such as 150 for 15 on a cut rule does exactly that. Set real floors before you use large cuts, and read the number again before you click <Ui>Add Rule</Ui>. Read [Floors and ceilings](/docs/review/floors-and-ceilings).

<Callout kind="careful" title="An amount of 0 cannot be saved">
The form accepts 0 as an amount, but a rule that changes the price by nothing is refused: "Enter a percent or a fixed amount." appears in red under the form. Type a real amount.
</Callout>

## Apply to room types

The <Ui>Apply to room types</Ui> field is a row of chips, one per active room type. A selected chip is highlighted. Above the chips a short summary reads "All room types", "K of N room types", "None selected" or, before your room types have loaded, "No room types loaded". The <Ui>Select all</Ui> and <Ui>Clear</Ui> buttons do what they say.

When the form opens, every room type that counts as a room is selected. A type you have unticked under <Ui>Counts as a room</Ui> on the <Ui>PMS</Ui> tab shows a small "not a room" tag on its chip, and hovering it reads "Meeting Room, not counted as a room (change this in the PMS tab)". It is left out of a new rule unless you click it. You can price it: a rule that changes the Meeting Room changes its price like any other type, and that is what lets MAYA send a price for it once you are live (a price you type for one of its nights on the calendar is sent too). What MAYA never does is measure it. Occupancy, booking speed and pickup are counted only over room types that count as rooms.

To keep a rule away from a room type, click its chip so it is no longer highlighted before you save. A rule with no room types is refused with "Select at least one room type."

With one list, the rule changes every selected type and measures the selected types that count as rooms. If you select only types that do not count as rooms, the rule can still change their prices but has nothing to measure. An occupancy, booking speed or pickup condition then never holds, so the rule never fires. A rule whose only condition is booking window still fires, because it counts days, not bookings. To move the Meeting Room's price with how busy your rooms are, use the split described next: measure your rooms and change the Meeting Room.

Under the chips is a checkbox, <Ui>Change prices on different room types</Ui>. Ticking it splits the field into a <Ui>Measure</Ui> list and a <Ui>Change</Ui> list, so a rule can watch your standard rooms and price your suites. Read [Measure one set of room types, change another](/docs/rules/measure-one-set-change-another).

## Add Rule, and what happens next

Click <Ui>Add Rule</Ui>. If everything is in order, a window opens, headed "Add "Last minute"?", with a small calendar of the months ahead, the days the new rule will change filled in, and "X days will be affected by this rule." Nothing is saved yet. Choose:

- <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui>: the rule is saved, switched <Ui>On</Ui>, and changes those days from the next pricing run
- <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui>: the rule is saved and <Ui>On</Ui>, but those days keep their prices until the rule stops being true on a day and then becomes true again. Every other day works as with Apply
- <Ui>Cancel</Ui>: nothing is saved, and the form keeps what you typed

When no price would change, the line reads "0 prices will be affected by this rule." and the only buttons are <Ui>Turn it on</Ui>, which saves the rule on, and <Ui>Cancel</Ui>. When you are saving changes to a rule that is on, that button reads <Ui>Save changes</Ui>. When the days cannot be worked out, it reads "We weren't able to calculate how many days would be affected by this rule." with <Ui>Try again</Ui>, <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> and <Ui>Cancel</Ui>.

Read [Turning a rule on, Apply or Skip](/docs/rules/turning-a-rule-on). Once saved:

- it appears in the list above, in the sort order the list uses (most fired first, then on before off, then by name)
- the form clears back to its defaults and stays open, ready for the next rule

The rule runs from the next pricing check, about five minutes away, on tonight and the next 395 nights, the days the window showed first. If the property is in simulation, the change log shows what it would have sent. If the property is live on Cloudbeds or ThinkReservations, changed prices go to your property system on the next cycle. On Mews nothing is sent, and the change log shows what the rule did.

<Ui>Save changes</Ui>, when you are editing, saves a new name alone at once, and a change to a rule that is <Ui>Off</Ui> at once too, moving no price. A change to a rule that is <Ui>On</Ui> opens the same window, headed "Save changes to "Last minute"?". Read [Change a rule](/docs/recipes/change-a-rule).

<Example title="Saving 'Last minute' at The Harbour Inn">
Priya, the Revenue Manager, wants a cut on quiet nights that are close. She types Last minute as the name. Row one: <Ui>Booking window (days to stay)</Ui>, <Ui>Less than</Ui>, 3. She clicks <Ui>+ Add condition</Ui>. The new row starts on <Ui>Sellable occupancy (%)</Ui>, because row one no longer uses it, and she sets it to <Ui>Less than</Ui>, 50. Direction <Ui>Decrease the rate</Ui>, percent 15. All three room types stay selected. She clicks <Ui>Add Rule</Ui>. The window shows two of the next three days filled in, "2 days will be affected by this rule.", and she clicks <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui>.

From the next check, a night 0, 1 or 2 days away with fewer than half its sellable rooms booked is lowered by 15%: Standard $150 becomes $127.50. A night that is 2 days away but 60% full is left alone, because both conditions must hold.
</Example>

## Who can add rules

Revenue Managers, General Managers and Hotel Admins can add, edit, switch and delete rules. A Viewer sees the same form and the same controls, and <Ui>Edit</Ui> fills the form so they can read a rule, but a Viewer's <Ui>Add Rule</Ui> or <Ui>Save changes</Ui> saves nothing: the window with the calendar opens for a moment and closes, and "Only a Revenue Manager or above can change this." appears in red under the form. The same line appears under a rule's switch or <Ui>Delete</Ui> button when a Viewer clicks them. Read [Roles: who can do what](/docs/team/roles).

<Callout kind="not-yet">
There is no date range, no weekday choice and no priority control in the builder, and editing a rule keeps its saved ones, such as the dates of a rule imported from PIE. Read [What rules cannot do yet](/docs/rules/what-rules-cannot-do-yet).
</Callout>
