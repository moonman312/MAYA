<InPlainWords>
Off freezes: the rule stops acting and the prices it already set stay exactly as they are. Delete undoes: every night it adjusted goes back to what it would have been. <Ui>Edit</Ui> opens a rule in the rule builder to change any of its settings. Before a rule starts acting, whether you switch it on or save changes to it while it is on, a window shows which days it will change and asks whether to apply or skip the price adjustments.
</InPlainWords>

## The On/Off switch

Each rule on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab has a switch under <Ui>Status</Ui> that reads <Ui>On</Ui> or <Ui>Off</Ui>.

- Switching <Ui>Off</Ui> is at once, with no confirmation, from the next cycle.
- Switching <Ui>On</Ui> first opens a window, headed "Turn on "Busy nights"?", with a small calendar of the days the rule will change and "X days will be affected by this rule." Click <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> and it acts on those days from the next cycle. Click <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> and it is on, but those days keep their prices until the rule stops being true on a day and then becomes true again; every other day works as with Apply. When no price would change, the window reads "0 prices will be affected by this rule." and <Ui>Turn it on</Ui> switches it on. <Ui>Cancel</Ui> leaves it <Ui>Off</Ui>. Read [Turning a rule on, Apply or Skip](/docs/rules/turning-a-rule-on).

A rule cannot be switched <Ui>On</Ui> while 40 others are on (read [How many rules you can have](#how-many-rules-you-can-have)).

Off means the rule stops acting. It does not undo anything:

- a standard rule's change that was on a night stays on that night, and does not come off when the condition stops holding, because the rule is no longer being checked
- a booking speed or pickup rule's raises and cuts stay on the price, and they are not checked for cancellations while it is off
- on the day cards and in your property system, nothing moves

Turn the rule back <Ui>On</Ui> and the window shows what that changes. With <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> it picks up from the next cycle: a standard rule that was holding a change lets it go where the condition no longer holds, and the rule acts wherever it holds. With <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> its changes on the days the window showed stay where they are, each until the rule stops being true there and then becomes true again, and every other night works as with Apply.

<Callout kind="good-to-know" title="A switched-off rule's changes still count">
Once a rule has changed a night, it counts only the bookings made since the newest change on that night by itself or by a stronger rule that moves the price the same way. A rule you switched off keeps its changes on the price, so its newest one still marks where it and the weaker rules start counting on that night. Read [Counting after a change](/docs/rules/how-rules-run#counting-after-a-change).
</Callout>

## Delete

The red <Ui>Delete</Ui> button at the end of a rule's row opens a dialog headed "Delete "Busy nights"?". It says that deleting the rule also undoes the price changes it has already made, and that any night it adjusted goes back to what the price would be if the rule had never run. Then, in amber: "Deleting cannot be undone." Three buttons:

- <Ui>Delete Rule and Undo Its Changes</Ui>: removes the rule and every change it made, from the next cycle
- <Ui>Turn It Off and Keep Its Changes</Ui>: switches the rule off and closes the dialog
- <Ui>Cancel</Ui>

The change log keeps its price entries, but older entries lose the deleted rule's reasons. A standard rule's move still shows, named "Pricing rule", with no sentence saying why. A booking speed or pickup rule's move is left out of those entries. Your answers to the rule's keeps-adjusting banner (the "Your answer" entries) are removed along with the rule. New entries show the price without it. Read [The change log](/docs/watch/the-change-log).

<Callout kind="careful">
Deleting a rule changes prices. Every night the rule had adjusted is re-priced without it from the next cycle, nearest nights first. On a live Cloudbeds or ThinkReservations property, those prices are sent to your system. On Mews they change in MAYA only. If you want the prices to stay where they are, turn the rule off instead.
</Callout>

## Changing a rule

Click <Ui>Edit</Ui> beside <Ui>Delete</Ui> at the end of the rule's row. The rule builder opens under the list, headed "Edit "Busy nights"", with the rule as it is saved: its name, its conditions, the direction and amount, the room types it measures and changes, and the undo box, <Ui>Undo the change if cancellations mean this rule is no longer true</Ui>. The rule's row is highlighted while you edit. Change what you want and click <Ui>Save changes</Ui>:

- **Only the name changed**: saved at once. A name moves no price.
- **The rule is On**: the window opens first, headed "Save changes to "Busy nights"?", with the days the edited rule will change. Apply, Skip or Cancel, as for switching a rule on. Cancel keeps what you typed in the builder.
- **The rule is Off**: saved at once, and no price moves. Its changes stay frozen, and the window shows what the edited rule does when you switch it on.

<Ui>Cancel editing</Ui>, under the form, empties the builder without saving. A rule's date window, weekdays and priority are not in the builder, and an edit keeps them as saved. If someone changed the rule in another tab after you opened it, the save stops with "This rule changed in another tab. Reload it to edit." and a <Ui>Reload</Ui> button that fills the builder with the rule as it is now.

**What an edit does to the rule's changes.** With <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui>, every change the rule has on the price is judged against the edited rule: a standard rule's change stays at the new amount where the edited rule holds and comes off where it does not (room types taken off the rule and nights it no longer covers included), and a booking speed or pickup rule's earlier changes come off, with the edited rule reading its whole window again. With <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui>, every change on the days the window showed stays where it is until the rule stops being true there and then becomes true again, and every other night is judged as with Apply. Changing its conditions, amount or room types also clears the rule's "Stop for this night" answers, which belonged to the rule as it was. A new name or the undo box alone keeps them. Read [A rule that is changed](/docs/rules/when-a-change-comes-off#a-rule-that-is-changed).

The "Tune ... ?" card after <Ui>Get suggestions from my data</Ui> is an edit too: <Ui>Make that change</Ui> moves the occupancy threshold of a standard rule, through the same window when the rule is on. Read [Getting suggestions later](/docs/review/getting-suggestions-later) and [Change a rule](/docs/recipes/change-a-rule).

<Callout kind="not-yet">
You cannot copy or duplicate a rule, or copy one to another property. For those, add the rule again. A rule you build in the <Ui>Rate Simulator</Ui> arrives on the Rules tab switched off.
</Callout>

## The list

The rules table has seven columns: <Ui>Name</Ui>, <Ui>Fired</Ui>, <Ui>Conditions</Ui>, <Ui>Room Types</Ui>, <Ui>Price Change</Ui>, <Ui>Status</Ui>, and the <Ui>Edit</Ui> and <Ui>Delete</Ui> buttons. On a narrow screen it scrolls sideways.

**Order.** Most fired in the last 90 days first, then rules that are on before rules that are off, then by name.

**Filter.** The pills above the table, <Ui>All</Ui>, <Ui>Enabled</Ui> and <Ui>Disabled</Ui>, show every rule, only the ones that are on, or only the ones that are off.

**Fired.** How many times the rule acted in the last 90 days, counted once per night and room type. A dash means not once in that time. A "?" beside the heading explains that it covers the last 90 days, that a booking speed or pickup rule can act on the same night more than once, and each time counts, that a change that came off later still counts, and that clicking the number shows each one. So the count is usually higher than the changes you can see on the calendar now, and higher than the change log shows, which lists the 40 biggest moves of each run and only counts the rest. A fire older than 90 days drops out of the count, so a rule that has not acted lately can go down, or back to a dash. Click the number to see each fire it counts ([The fire log](#the-fire-log)).

**Conditions.** In words: "Occupancy above 80%", "Booking window below 7 days", "Pickup above 5 bookings (past 3 days), then waits 3 days", "booking speed at least Faster Than Normal (past week), then waits 1 week". Several are joined with a dot.

**Room Types.** The types the rule changes, or "Watches Standard, Deluxe · Changes Suite" when it measures different ones.

**Price Change.** "+10%", "-15%", "+$25", "-$25". The sign in this column is always a dollar sign, whatever your property's currency. The amount is in your currency. The change log and the keeps-adjusting banner use your currency's own symbol.

**Stopped on N nights.** An amber chip under the switch when you have told the rule to stop on some upcoming nights, with a "?" and a <Ui>Let it run again</Ui> link. Read [Rules that keep adjusting](/docs/rules/rules-that-keep-adjusting).

## The fire log

Click a rule's number in the <Ui>Fired</Ui> column ("12×") and a window opens with the rule's name at the top, "12 fires in the last 90 days" under it, and a "?" that explains the count as the list's does and says the times are your property's. It lists exactly the fires the number counts, newest first, 25 at a time, one row each:

- when it fired, in your property's time zone: "Oct 3, 2:05 PM", with the year when it was not this year. Hover it for the exact second and the zone.
- the night it changed: "Fri Nov 13".
- what it did, in your currency: "+10%", "+$15". A small amber <Ui>Simulation</Ui> tag sits beside it when your property was simulating at the time.

If a fire landed since the rules list loaded, the window's count is the newer one, and the list's number changes to match it.

**Click a row** to open it, and click again to close it. It shows:

- **The price.** The room type and the night's price before and after the run that made the fire: "Queen: $150.00 to $165.00." It is the night's whole price, so another rule acting in the same run is in it too. With no earlier price on record (a night new to the window) it reads "Queen: $165.00 after this run.", and when the price did not move, "Queen: stayed at $165.00." A fire made while simulating reads "Simulation: the price for Fri Nov 13, Queen would have gone from $150.00 to $165.00." When your floor or ceiling held the price, "It stopped at your ceiling." follows ("It would have stopped" in simulation). When the record of that run is not there, it says "The prices for this run aren't on record."
- **Where the price went**, in the change log's words. A fire made while simulating says "Nothing was sent to Cloudbeds.", and still does after you go live. A live fire whose price is still the night's latest says "Sent to Cloudbeds.", "Waiting to be sent to Cloudbeds.", "Couldn't be sent to Cloudbeds." or "Held back, not sent to Cloudbeds.", from MAYA's record of its sends. A fire whose price a later run replaced has no such line: MAYA's record keeps only each night's latest send, so it cannot say. On Mews a live fire says "Nothing was sent to Mews. MAYA doesn't send prices there yet." With no record of which mode your property was in at the time, nothing is said about sending.
- **Why it fired**, in the change log's sentences with the numbers it saw: "It was 72% full, past the 70% mark you set." For a booking speed or pickup rule the numbers come from the run that fired it, and if that record is missing, from what the fire kept itself, numbers only: "6 bookings arrived in its count." If you have edited the rule since, only the numbers show, then "The rule has been edited since.", because its marks at the time are not on record.
- **What happened later**, when anything did, oldest first: "Came off Oct 5, 9:10 AM." (a standard rule's change, when its conditions stopped holding or an edit took it off), "Came off Oct 5, 9:10 AM: bookings behind it cancelled.", ": a price was set by hand." or ": the rule was edited.", "Moved to the rule's new amount Oct 5, 9:10 AM." (an edit kept the change at the rule's new amount), "A price set by hand took over Oct 5, 9:10 AM." and "Told to stop on this night Oct 5, 9:10 AM." A rate changed in your property system on a night MAYA sent counts as a price set by hand. Each is worded for the mode at its own time: a change that came off while simulating "Would have come off".

When the rule is off, "This rule is off. Its changes stay where they are." shows under its name. A rule with no fires in the last 90 days shows a dash in the list, with nothing to click.

**Older** under the rows adds the 25 fires before them. While they load it reads "Loading…", and if they cannot load, "Couldn't load older fires." shows beside it. Once the oldest is on screen, "Nothing older. Fires are kept for 90 days." shows instead. If the window cannot load at all it says "Couldn't load this rule's fires." with a <Ui>Try again</Ui> link. Close it with the × at the top right, the Escape key, or a click outside it.

**What a fire is.** The count and the window read the same list, so they always agree: each time a standard rule's change went on a night and room type, and each raise or cut a booking speed or pickup rule made, in the last 90 days. That is how long MAYA keeps the record that explains a fire. Nothing is read until you click, so the rules list loads as fast as before. Everyone who can see the rules list sees the same window, whatever their role, and there is nothing in it to change.

## How many rules you can have

At most 40 rules can be <Ui>On</Ui> at once on a property. Rules that are <Ui>Off</Ui> do not count, and a rule saved from the <Ui>Rate Simulator</Ui> arrives Off. At 40, the window with the calendar opens for a moment when you add a rule or switch an Off rule on, then closes, and "This property already has 40 active rules, which is the maximum." shows in red under the form or under the rule's switch. Nothing is saved. Turn a rule off or delete one first.

Every occupancy or booking window rule whose condition holds applies, and their changes stack. Booking speed and pickup rules take turns: at most one fires on a night and room type per run. Read [How rules run](/docs/rules/how-rules-run).

## Who can do this

Revenue Managers, General Managers and Hotel Admins can switch, edit and delete rules. A Viewer sees the switch and the <Ui>Edit</Ui> and <Ui>Delete</Ui> buttons, and can open a rule in the builder to read it, but switching, saving or deleting changes nothing and shows "Only a Revenue Manager or above can change this." in red under the rule or the form. Read [Roles: who can do what](/docs/team/roles).

## Who did what

The app does not show who added, switched or deleted a rule. If you need to know, [email us](mailto:info@modern-hospitality-solutions.com?subject=Who%20changed%20a%20rule) with the rule's name and roughly when.

The change log has no entry for adding a rule, editing it, switching it on or off, or deleting it, or for which of Apply or Skip was chosen: it shows only what those steps did to prices. The people the change log does name are whoever typed a price ("Priya set the base rate to $180.00.") and whoever answered the keeps-adjusting banner (the "Your answer" entries).
