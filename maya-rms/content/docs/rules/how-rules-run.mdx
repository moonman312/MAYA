<InPlainWords>
About every five minutes MAYA reads your bookings and checks every rule again on each night whose bookings changed, for each room type. Once a day, just after midnight at your property, it checks every rule on every night from today to 395 days out. Standard rules (occupancy, booking window) switch on while their condition holds and off when it stops. Booking speed and pickup rules fire, keep their change on the price, wait, and can fire again on the bookings made since. Percent changes compound, fixed amounts add, and the result stays between the floor and ceiling.
</InPlainWords>

## The cycle

A cycle is one run of MAYA for your property. It comes round about every five minutes. Each cycle:

1. reads your bookings from your property system
2. refreshes your own rates when they are due (Cloudbeds and ThinkReservations)
3. runs every rule, for every room type, on the nights that need it: every night whose bookings changed since the last cycle, every night a change of yours reaches, and, in the first cycles after midnight at your property, every night in the window, nearest nights first
4. sends the prices that changed, if your property is live (Cloudbeds and ThinkReservations)

A change you make that can move any night (a rule, a room type setting, a closed period, a "not a fair comparison" flag) runs the rules on every night again from the next cycle, nearest first. Switching a rule on or saving one also puts the days its window showed ahead of the rest. A typed price, rooms out of service and your answer on the three-changes banner run them on those nights. A change to a stay already over reaches future prices with the next day's pass.

Waits and lookback windows count whole days at your property, so time passing never changes a price during the day: a night nothing happened to keeps its price until the next day's pass.

On Mews, a cycle reads your bookings and runs your rules, and that is all. Your own rates are not read and nothing is sent, live or not. Read [Nothing is sent to Mews](/docs/connect/mews#nothing-is-sent-to-mews).

Typing a price on the calendar runs the rules again on those nights at once, without waiting for the next cycle. If a cycle cannot read your bookings, it prices nothing and sends nothing, and the next cycle tries again.

The <Ui>Change Log</Ui> tab lists each cycle that changed a price as a pricing run, with one sentence per change. Read [The change log](/docs/watch/the-change-log).

## The window

Your rules price tonight and the next 395 nights: 396 nights in all, about 13 months. "Tonight" is the calendar date at your property, in your property's time zone, at the moment the cycle runs.

- Tonight is night 0 and is always priced.
- A night 395 days away is the last one in the window. A night 396 or more days away is not in it, which is why a rule can fire 395 days out and not 397. The night comes into the window at your property's midnight and is priced in that day's pass.
- Past nights are never priced or re-priced. Once your property's date moves past a night, every change on it is left as it was.
- A price you type further ahead (up to 399 days) is saved now. Your rules price it, and it is sent, once the night enters the window.

## Two kinds of rule

The <Ui>How rules behave</Ui> panel on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab shows three short animations: a booking speed rule that raises and waits, an occupancy rule with no wait, and a stronger rule covering a weaker one. A checkbox at the top, "Try the box", plays them with the undo box ticked or unticked. Under them the panel says the box works the same way on every rule, raise or cut, and that turning a rule off keeps its changes as they are.

| | Standard rule | Booking speed or pickup rule |
|---|---|---|
| Conditions | Only sellable occupancy and booking window | Has a booking speed or pickup count row (with or without the others) |
| How it acts | Switches on when its condition starts to hold, keeps one change on the price while it holds, switches off when it stops | Fires, and the change stays on the price when the condition stops holding |
| Same night twice | Never. One change while the condition holds | Yes. After its wait, if the bookings made since keep it true, it fires again and the changes stack |
| When the change comes off | When its condition stops holding. With its undo box unticked, falling occupancy never takes it off | With its undo box ticked, when cancellations mean the rule is no longer true. Either way, when you set a price, the night passes or you delete the rule |

Read [When a price change comes off](/docs/rules/when-a-change-comes-off) for the undo box and every other way a change comes off.

## What a rule starts from

Every night and room type has a base price, and rules are applied on top of it. MAYA takes the first of these that exists:

1. a price a person set for that night: typed in MAYA, or changed in your property system on a night MAYA had already sent to
2. your own rate for the night, read from your property system
3. the rate of the newest booking for that night and room type
4. the base saved for the night on the last run

On Mews, your own rates are not read and a rate changed in Mews is never read back, so the base is a price you typed in MAYA, else the newest booking's rate, else the base saved on the last run. Read [Where a night's starting price comes from](/docs/connect/mews#where-a-nights-starting-price-comes-from).

If your system shows the night's rate as 0 (a closed night, for example) and you have not typed a price, MAYA leaves the night unpriced. No booking speed or pickup rule fires on it, nothing is sent and the calendar shows a dash for its price. This applies on Cloudbeds and ThinkReservations, where MAYA reads your rates.

Standard rules still switch on and off on that night as their condition starts and stops holding, and each switch-on counts in <Ui>Fired</Ui>. So once the night has a rate, any standard rule already on there applies straight away, and the change log shows it from then.

If your system sends no rate at all for the night (blank, not 0), MAYA uses the next starting point in the list above instead. And a 0 you typed yourself is a real price: a comp night. Read [Setting a price yourself](/docs/watch/setting-a-price-yourself).

## Standard rules, step by step

For each night and room type:

- **Activate.** The conditions hold and the rule was not active there. The change goes on the price. This counts as one fire in the <Ui>Fired</Ui> column.
- **Hold.** The conditions still hold. Nothing changes, and the rule does not fire again.
- **Deactivate.** The conditions stop holding. The change comes off and the price goes back.

So a cancellation that drops a night from 82% to 78% takes a "Greater than 80" raise off on the next cycle. A late booking that lifts it back above 80 puts it on again, and that counts as another fire. With the rule's undo box unticked, a <Ui>Greater than</Ui> occupancy condition counts as met for as long as the change is on, so falling occupancy never takes the raise off. A booking window condition still runs out as the night gets closer.

## Booking speed and pickup rules, step by step

- **Fire.** The conditions hold, the rule is not waiting on that night and room type, and it wins the competition (below). The change goes on the price. One fire.
- **Wait.** The rule leaves that night and room type alone for its <Ui>Then waits (advanced)</Ui> setting, 1 day to 2 weeks. A booking speed row starts at 1 week. A pickup count row starts on <Ui>Same as the lookback window</Ui>. A rule with both waits the longer of the two. The wait counts whole days at your property: a 2-day wait from a fire at any time on Monday is over as Wednesday begins, and the rule reads the night again in the first cycle of that day.
- **Read again.** When the wait is over, the rule reads the night again, counting only the bookings made since the newest change on that night by itself or by a stronger rule that moves the price the same way, while that change is still on the price. Changes made by rules you have switched off count too, because they stay on the price. If those bookings keep the rule true, it fires again and the change stacks on the earlier one. Read [Counting after a change](#counting-after-a-change).
- **Tell you at three.** Once three of a rule's changes are on the same night, an amber banner asks you whether to keep going. Read [Rules that keep adjusting](/docs/rules/rules-that-keep-adjusting).

The change log shows a repeat as "Then "Hot-week surge" raised it another 25%, from $250.00 to $312.50."

<Example title="A 2-day wait after a Monday fire">
The starter rule "Hot-week surge" is <Ui>Much Faster Than Normal</Ui> or faster over <Ui>Past week</Ui>, waits <Ui>2 days</Ui>, raises 25%. It fires on the Saturday's price on Monday afternoon.

Tuesday: waiting. Wednesday, just after midnight: the wait is over. The rule now counts the Saturday's bookings that came in after Monday's raise, from Monday afternoon on, and through Wednesday as they come. Because it raises on a fast pace, those bookings alone have to read Much Faster Than Normal or faster against what similar Saturdays get in a whole week. It never re-counts the bookings that made it fire on Monday.

If Monday's burst was the whole story, the bookings since the raise read Normal and the price holds at one raise. If bookings keep coming, it raises again on Wednesday, waits until Friday, and so on. At three raises on that night, MAYA asks you.
</Example>

**"My rule's wait is over and the night still reads faster, so why did it not act again?"** Because the whole-window reading you see in an older log entry includes the bookings the rule already acted on. After a change, the rule only counts what arrived since. If those bookings alone do not keep it true, it does nothing. That is by design: your rules never stack two raises on the same bookings.

## Counting after a change

Once a rule has changed a night, it counts only the bookings made since the newest change on that night by itself or by a stronger rule that moves the price the same way. A weaker rule's change never moves where a stronger rule counts from, and raises and cuts never move each other's count. A change that has come off no longer counts as a starting point.

- **A rule that raises** counts from the raise itself: on the day of the raise, only the bookings that reached MAYA after it, then every day since. A rule that raises on a fast pace (<Ui>Faster Than Normal</Ui>, <Ui>Much Faster Than Normal</Ui> or <Ui>Surging</Ui>) needs those bookings alone to beat what similar nights get in its whole window. Any other booking speed rule compares them with similar nights over the same days.
- **A rule that cuts** counts full days only, up to yesterday, on the night and on the nights it is compared with. After a cut it counts the full days after the day of the cut, so it has nothing to judge until one of those has passed.
- **A pickup rule** counts, inside its lookback window, only the room nights (or revenue) booked after the change and still booked. One set to <Ui>Less than</Ui> counts full days only, up to yesterday, so its last change counted the days before its own. It judges again once a whole lookback window of full days, starting with the day of that change, has passed, because a short stretch always looks slow.
- **When cancellations hit a change** and bookings made since keep its rule true, the change stays, and every rule keeps counting from when it was made. Counting starts again only when a price changes.
- **A price you type** starts every rule over: each waits its wait from your price, then reads its whole window again.
- **After you skip.** A rule switched on or saved with <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> counts from the moment you clicked, or from its own newest change still on the night when that is later: a raise the rest of that day and the days after, a cut from the next full day, a pickup count the room nights booked after your click. Skipping starts no wait and moves no other rule's count, since no price changed. An edit applied with <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> starts a booking speed or pickup rule on its whole window again. Read [Turning a rule on, Apply or Skip](/docs/rules/turning-a-rule-on#skip-price-adjustments).

<Example title="Two tiers at The Harbour Inn">
"Five" raises 10% on 5 or more room nights of pickup in a week. "Ten" raises 20% on 10 or more. Standard starts at $100. On Monday 10 book at once. Both rules are true, and "Ten" changes the price by more, so it raises: $120. "Five" does not add its 10% on the same bookings.

On Tuesday 3 more book, and on Wednesday one of Monday's guests cancels. "Ten" still has 12 in its week, so its raise stays, and both rules keep counting from Monday's raise. On Thursday 2 more book: 5 since the raise, so "Five" raises 10% on top: $132. "Ten" still counts from its own raise, 5 so far, because a weaker rule's raise never restarts a stronger rule's count.
</Example>

## Which one fires when several match

At most one booking speed or pickup rule fires on a given night and room type in one cycle. When two or more are true and not waiting, the stronger one fires. The first step where they differ settles it:

1. The bigger change to the price, in money on that night's price, comes first (10% of $200 is $20, which beats $15).
2. At the same change, a rule with a booking speed condition comes before one without, and of two with one, the faster speed comes first for a raise (the slower speed for a cut).
3. Next, the pickup count that is harder to meet comes first (<Ui>Greater than</Ui> 6 before <Ui>Greater than</Ui> 4).
4. Next, starter rules come before rules you build, in this order: Sudden-spike catcher, Hot-week surge, Warm-date bump, Slow-date rescue, Slow-date trim. Every rule you build ranks the same at this step.
5. Next, the rule with more conditions comes first.
6. Last, the older rule comes first.

That order is what "stronger" means everywhere in MAYA, including where a rule counts from. The "?" panels in the rule builder give the short version: a stronger rule changes the price by more, and at the same change, a rule that watches booking speed is stronger, set to the faster speed (the slower one, for cuts).

A stronger rule that is still waiting can hold the night. It holds a weaker rule that moves the price the same way only when it would be true again on what it counts itself, the bookings since its own latest change. Then nothing fires on that night this cycle. It holds a rule that moves the price the other way while its whole window still matches. A stronger rule can always fire while a weaker one waits.

<Example title="Two raise rules on one Saturday">
You built "Busy week": <Ui>Faster Than Normal</Ui> over <Ui>Past week</Ui>, raise 10%. On Monday, 12 bookings arrive for Saturday, November 14, 2026, where a night like it usually gets 5 in a week. "Busy week" and the starter rule "Hot-week surge" (<Ui>Much Faster Than Normal</Ui>, raise 25%) are both true. Hot-week surge changes the price by more, so it raises the Saturday and "Busy week" does not.

Hot-week surge then waits 2 days, and both rules count only the bookings made after its raise. If 9 more arrive by Tuesday, that reads faster than normal against the usual 5 for a week, so "Busy week" raises 10% on top. If 10 or more arrive, Hot-week surge would be true again on its own count, so it holds the night and "Busy week" cannot fire under it.
</Example>

Before ranking, a rule is dropped for that night when it could not move the price: a raise on a night already at its ceiling, a cut already at its floor, a percent on a price of 0, or any raise on a night you set to 0.

Standard rules do not compete. Every standard rule whose condition holds applies.

## Putting the price together

For each night and room type, MAYA starts from the base price and applies:

1. every active standard rule change, in a fixed order that is not the order you made them (this only matters when a percent rule and a fixed-amount rule both hold on the same night)
2. every open booking speed and pickup fire, in the order they were made
3. the floor and ceiling

Percent changes multiply: $200 raised 10% is $220, and 25% on top of that is $275, not $270. Fixed amounts add: $200 plus $25 is $225. The result is rounded to cents, and that is the only rounding there is. There is no setting to round to whole numbers or to make prices end in 9. For a price such as $199, type it yourself. Rules that fire later still stack on it and round to the cent. If it would go above the room type's ceiling, the ceiling is published. If it would go below the floor, the floor is.

You can only type a price inside the floor and ceiling, or 0 for a comp night. If a limit changes later, a price you already typed stays as you typed it. Rules on top of it are held between the limits, stretched to include your price. Say Standard's floor was $60 when you typed $70, and you later raised the floor to $80. Your $70 stays. A cut rule can take it no lower than $70, and a raise can go up to the $300 ceiling.

<Example title="Stacking at The Harbour Inn">
Deluxe is $200, floor $110, ceiling $400. "Busy nights" (a standard rule) raises 10%: $220. The starter rule "Hot-week surge" (booking speed) fires: 25% on $220 is $275. The change log reads:

"Busy nights" raised this night 10%, from $200.00 to $220.00. It was 85% full, past the 80% mark you set. Then "Hot-week surge" raised it 25%, from $220.00 to $275.00. Bookings came in much faster than normal this past week: 12, against the 5 a night like this usually has by now.

Now a festival night, with Standard's rate at $290 and its ceiling at $300: "Busy nights" asks for $319. The change log adds "That would have gone past your $300.00 ceiling for Standard, so it stopped there." and $300 is published. "Hot-week surge" does not fire on Standard at all, because a raise cannot fire on a night already at its ceiling. Read [Floors and ceilings](/docs/review/floors-and-ceilings).
</Example>

## How often a price changes

Your rules check a night within about five minutes of its bookings changing, and every night in the window once a day, and a night's price changes only when something changes it, and it is sent to your property system only when it changes (Cloudbeds and ThinkReservations, once you are live). On a quiet property, hours can pass with nothing sent. Guests do not see a price bouncing every five minutes.

What can move a night's price, and how often:

- **A standard rule** changes it once when its condition starts to hold and once when it stops. A booking window rule starts to hold as the night gets closer. An occupancy rule on a night that hovers around its line can switch on and off as bookings arrive and cancel.
- **A booking speed or pickup rule** changes it at most once per wait. At most one of these rules fires on a night and room type each cycle. With its undo box ticked, cancellations can also take one of its changes off.
- **Three changes** by the same rule on the same night bring the <Ui>Rules that keep adjusting</Ui> banner, which asks whether it should keep going.
- **You**, when you type a price, and **your own rate**, when it changes in your property system.

So a night can still change more than once in a day when bookings come and go, but never only because time passed: a wait or a lookback window moves on only at your property's midnight.

## Per room type

Everything above happens separately for each room type the rule changes. Waits run per night and room type. Floors and ceilings are per room type. So a rule can fire on Deluxe and not on Standard on the same night: Standard may still be waiting, sitting at its ceiling, or measured differently. The <Ui>Fired</Ui> column counts every time the rule acted, once per night and room type each time.

## Deleted or switched-off rules

Turning a rule off freezes what it has done: its changes stay on the price, cancellations do not take them off, and nothing new happens. An edit saved while it is off changes nothing either, until you switch it on. Deleting a rule undoes everything it did from the next cycle. Read [Turning a rule off, deleting it, or changing it](/docs/rules/off-delete-or-change).

## Where you see all this

The <Ui>Change Log</Ui> tab lists every pricing run that changed a price, with the chain of rules and running prices for each room type and night, so stacking is visible. Under a booking speed change, <Ui>How did we know?</Ui> opens the counts behind it. Read [The change log](/docs/watch/the-change-log) and ["How did we know?" explained](/docs/watch/how-did-we-know).

<Callout kind="careful">
In simulation everything above runs and is written to the change log, but nothing is sent. After you go live on Cloudbeds or ThinkReservations, changed prices go to your property system every cycle. Nothing is sent to Mews. Read [Simulation mode](/docs/live/simulation-mode).
</Callout>

<Callout kind="not-yet">
The cycle length and the 396-night window are the same for every property and cannot be changed from the app. Rules do not check a night further out than the window. For such a night, type a price (up to 399 days ahead). It is priced and sent when the night enters the window. There is no rounding or price-ending setting either: prices are rounded to the cent.
</Callout>
