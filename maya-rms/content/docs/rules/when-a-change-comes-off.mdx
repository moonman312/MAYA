<InPlainWords>
A standard rule's change comes off when its condition stops holding. A booking speed or pickup rule's change comes off when cancellations mean the rule is no longer true, as long as its undo box is ticked, which it is on every new rule. A cut on a slow pace never comes off because guests cancel. Your typed price takes earlier changes off that night. Deleting a rule undoes everything it did. Turning a rule off undoes nothing.
</InPlainWords>

## The undo box

Every rule has one box, <Ui>Undo the change if cancellations mean this rule is no longer true</Ui>, under <Ui>Rate adjustment</Ui> in the rule builder. It starts ticked on every new rule, and starter rules and accepted suggestions are ticked too. On the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab it shows under each rule's switch as <Ui>Undo on cancellations</Ui>, where a Revenue Manager or above can tick or untick it without changing anything else about the rule. Anyone else who tries sees "Only a Revenue Manager or above can change this." The "?" beside the box sums up this page.

- **Ticked.** When a booking that a change counted cancels, MAYA checks the rule again. If the rule is no longer true for that night, the change comes off. If bookings made since keep it true, the change stays.
- **Unticked.** Cancellations never take the change off.

The box works the same way for a raise or a cut and for every kind of condition. Cancellations can only make a rule untrue through a <Ui>Greater than</Ui> occupancy or pickup line, or a booking speed the rule needs at least. A booking window, anything <Ui>Less than</Ui> and a slow pace only get truer as guests cancel, so a cut made because a night was booking slowly stays when cancellations slow it down further.

Once a change has come off, its rule can change the night again when it is true again and its wait is over. The wait still runs from the change that came off, so a night on the edge does not go up and down every cycle. A standard rule has no wait, so it goes back on as soon as it holds again.

Either way, a change also comes off when the night passes, when a price is set by you or in your property system, and when you delete the rule.

## Standard rules

A standard rule (only <Ui>Sellable occupancy (%)</Ui> and <Ui>Booking window (days to stay)</Ui> rows) holds one change on a night and room type while its condition holds, and takes it off on the first cycle where it does not. With the undo box ticked, that includes a night that drops under its occupancy line because guests cancelled.

<Example title="A cancellation at The Harbour Inn">
"Busy nights" raises 10% when a night is more than 80% full. Friday is 17 of 20 booked (85%): Standard is $165. Two guests cancel, so Friday drops to 15 of 20 (75%). On the next cycle, about five minutes later, the raise comes off and Standard goes back to $150. If a new booking lifts Friday over 80% again, the raise goes back on.
</Example>

With the box unticked, a <Ui>Greater than</Ui> occupancy condition counts as met for as long as the change is on, so falling occupancy never takes the raise off. The rest of the rule is read as the night is now: a booking window condition still runs out as the night gets closer, and a <Ui>Less than</Ui> occupancy rule still switches off when bookings lift the night over its line.

The <Ui>How rules behave</Ui> panel on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab animates both. Ticked: "Occupancy falls to 65%, so the rule is no longer true and its raise comes off." Unticked: "Occupancy falls to 65%. The box is unticked, so the raise stays."

## Changes after cancellations

A booking speed or pickup rule's change stays on the price after the rule fires. With the undo box ticked, cancellations take it off only when they make the rule no longer true. The check starts once a room that was booked for the night when the change was made has cancelled. It recounts what the change counted, not the night as it is now:

| The rule's condition | What the check looks at |
|---|---|
| Booking speed | The bookings the change counted that are still booked, against what a night like it usually got over those days, as read when the change was made. The rule's own level is the bar |
| Pickup count <Ui>Greater than</Ui> | The pickup the change counted, less the bookings from that stretch that have cancelled since |
| Sellable occupancy <Ui>Greater than</Ui> | The night's sellable occupancy now |

If the change's own count falls short, MAYA reads the rule once more the way it would count with the change off, bookings made since included. If that keeps the rule true, the change stays and every rule keeps counting from when it was made. Only when both fall short does the change come off.

Each stacked change is checked on its own numbers. A change is never checked in the cycle that made it, never while its rule is switched off, and never once the rule measures a different set of room types from the one it measured when it fired.

<Example title="Two cancellations, then one more">
On Monday, October 12, 2026, "Hot-week surge" raised Saturday, November 14, 2026 by 25%, after 12 bookings arrived in a week when a night like it usually gets 5. Two of those 12 cancel: 10 are still booked, still much faster than normal, so the raise stays. One more cancels: 9 left read only faster than normal, short of the Much Faster Than Normal the rule needs. Unless the Saturday's week, read as it stands now, still reads Much Faster Than Normal, the raise comes off on the next cycle.
</Example>

The change log then says: Cancellations meant "Hot-week surge" was no longer true, so its 25% raise came off. Of the 12 bookings it counted, 9 are still booked, where nights like it usually get about 5, and the rule needs a booking speed of at least much faster than normal. When nothing else is left on the night, the entry ends with the move back, such as "That took this night from $250.00 to $200.00."

## Cuts

A cut rule whose conditions are a slow pace, a pickup count <Ui>Less than</Ui> or a booking window never comes off because of how the night books. Guests cancelling only make it truer, and new bookings are what the cut was for: a cut that switched off the moment a booking arrived would undo the thing that brought the booking in. If bookings have recovered and you want the price back up, type the price you want on the calendar, or delete the rule, which undoes every cut it made.

**"Bookings recovered, so why did the cut not come off?"** Because bookings arriving never take a cut off. A cut rule can keep cutting the same night after each wait, down to your floor. Once three of its cuts are on the night the amber banner asks whether it should keep going, and until you answer, it does. Read [Rules that keep adjusting](/docs/rules/rules-that-keep-adjusting).

## A price you type

When you type a price for a night and room type on the calendar:

- every booking speed and pickup change already on that night and room type comes off ("this night's price was set by hand" in the change log)
- every standard rule change already there is held: the rule stays on, so it does not fire again on the same trigger, but it stops moving the price until its condition next changes
- your number becomes the base, and rules that fire later stack on top of it
- a booking speed or pickup rule that already existed waits its wait from the moment you saved, then reads its whole window again

Read [Setting a price yourself](/docs/watch/setting-a-price-yourself). Clearing your price hands the night back: held standard rules move the price again, but the booking speed and pickup changes that came off stay off.

<Callout kind="careful" title="Typing a price is the quickest undo">
If a price looks wrong and you want it fixed now, type the price you want. It is saved at once. If you are live on Cloudbeds or ThinkReservations it is sent right away or on the next cycle, about five minutes later (the screen says which). A night more than 59 nights ahead is sent when it enters the 60-night window. A 0 is not sent, and nothing is sent to Mews. Read [Undo a price change](/docs/recipes/undo-a-price-change).
</Callout>

## A rule that is changed

Today there is no edit control for a rule. The one change you can make in place is accepting a "Tune ... ?" suggestion after <Ui>Get suggestions from my data</Ui>, which moves the occupancy threshold of a standard rule. From the next cycle the rule checks every night against the new threshold, so a night that no longer meets it loses the change, and a night that now meets it gains one. Ticking or unticking the undo box is not a change to the rule: its changes stay where they are.

## The night passes

Once your property's date moves past a night, every change on it is left as it was and nothing touches it again. Past nights are never re-priced.

## A deleted rule

Deleting a rule removes every change it made, whether from a standard rule or a booking speed or pickup rule, from the next cycle. Every night it adjusted goes back to what the price would be if the rule had never run. The delete dialog says so and offers <Ui>Turn It Off and Keep Its Changes</Ui> beside <Ui>Delete Rule and Undo Its Changes</Ui>. Read [Turning a rule off, deleting it, or changing it](/docs/rules/off-delete-or-change).

Turning a rule off undoes nothing: its changes stay exactly as they are, and cancellations do not take them off while it is off.

## The change log sentences

| What happened | What you read |
|---|---|
| A standard rule switched off | If another rule or a typed price is still on the night, its next entry shows the price without the change, and no rule is named for the drop. If it was the only change: "Busy nights" stopped applying an earlier 10% raise here: this night no longer met its conditions. That took this night from $165.00 to $150.00. |
| A change came off for cancellations | Cancellations meant "Warm-date bump" was no longer true, so its 10% raise came off. Then the numbers the check found, and the move back when nothing else is left on the night |
| You typed a price | "Warm-date bump" stopped applying an earlier 10% raise here: this night's price was set by hand. Then a line naming who set it. |
| A rule was deleted | The next run prices without it. In older entries a deleted standard rule is called "Pricing rule" with no reason given, and a deleted booking speed or pickup rule's line is left out |

**"Why did the price go back to the base rate?"** One of: a standard rule's condition stopped holding, cancellations meant a rule was no longer true, you or a colleague cleared a typed price, or the rule was deleted. The change log lists the night in the run that did it: it names what came off, then "That took this night from" the old price to the new one. When another rule or a typed price is still on the night, its entry shows the price without the change, and names a change that came off for cancellations.

<Callout kind="not-yet">
There is no undo button for one price change. The three ways back are: type the price you want on that night (now), delete the rule (undoes everything it did, everywhere), or stop the rule on that night from the banner once three of its changes are on it (no more changes, and what it did stays unless cancellations take it off).
</Callout>
