<InPlainWords>
Click <Ui>Edit</Ui> on the rule, change what you want in the rule builder, and click <Ui>Save changes</Ui>. A new name saves at once. If the rule is on, a window first shows the days the changed rule will affect: <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> changes them from the next pricing run, <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> leaves their prices as they are until the rule stops being true on a day and then becomes true again. If the rule is off, the change saves at once and moves no price until you switch it on.
</InPlainWords>

## Edit it

<Steps>
<Step title="Open it in the builder">
On the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab, click <Ui>Edit</Ui> at the end of the rule's row. The rule builder opens under the list, headed "Edit "Busy nights"", filled with the rule as saved: name, conditions, direction and amount, the room types it measures and changes, and the undo box. The row stays highlighted while you edit.
</Step>
<Step title="Change what you want">
Any field the builder has: a condition's threshold, speed or window, the amount, the room types, the undo box. A rule's date window, weekdays and priority are not in the builder, and are kept as saved. A booking speed condition saved as exactly a level below or above Normal, as some starter rules are, keeps its compare unless you pick another speed.
</Step>
<Step title="Save changes">
Click <Ui>Save changes</Ui>. A new name alone saves at once. For a rule that is <Ui>On</Ui>, the window opens with the days the changed rule will affect. Choose <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> or <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui>, or <Ui>Cancel</Ui> to keep editing. For a rule that is <Ui>Off</Ui>, the change saves at once. If the rule was changed in another tab since you opened it, the save stops with "This rule changed in another tab. Reload it to edit." Click <Ui>Reload</Ui>, check the rule and save again.
</Step>
</Steps>

<OpenInMaya to="rules.list" words="Open the Rules tab" />

<Ui>Cancel editing</Ui>, under the form, empties the builder without saving. Rules need Revenue Manager access or higher.

## What Apply and Skip do after an edit

**Apply price adjustments.** Every change the rule has on the price is judged against the edited rule from the next pricing run. A standard rule's change stays, at the new amount, where the edited rule holds, and comes off where it does not, on room types you took off the rule and nights it no longer covers too. A booking speed or pickup rule's earlier changes come off ("the rule was edited, so MAYA started it fresh" in the change log), and the edited rule reads its whole window again, with no wait. The days the window showed are the ones whose prices move.

**Skip price adjustments.** The days the window showed keep their prices, each until the rule stops being true there and then becomes true again. Every other day works as Apply. On those days a standard rule's change stays at its old amount: where the edited rule is true it stays, still stays once the rule stops being true (undo box ticked or not), and moves to the new amount the next time the rule is true; where the edited rule is not true, it waits for the rule to be true there and then moves to the new amount. On a room type or night you took off the rule, it comes off at that point instead. A booking speed or pickup rule's changes on those days stay, are not checked for cancellations, and still cover the rules they covered; once the rule has stopped and started being true on such a day, they come off and the edited rule acts there in that same run. Either way the edited rule counts every booking on the books, as it would with Apply.

Changing the conditions, amount or room types also clears the rule's "Stop for this night" answers: they belonged to the rule as it was. Read [Turning a rule on, Apply or Skip](/docs/rules/turning-a-rule-on) and [A rule that is changed](/docs/rules/when-a-change-comes-off#a-rule-that-is-changed).

<Example title="Moving 'Busy nights' from 80% to 90% at The Harbour Inn">
"Busy nights" raises Standard 10%, from $150 to $165, on nights more than 80% full. Priya edits it to more than 90%. Friday is 17 of 20 booked (85%), Saturday 19 of 20 (95%). With <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui>, the window shows Friday among the affected days: on the next run Friday goes back to $150 and Saturday keeps $165. With <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui>, both stay at $165. Friday's raise then waits until Friday is over 90% full, and Saturday's comes off when Saturday drops to 90% or below.
</Example>

## Accept a Tune suggestion

When you click <Ui>Get suggestions from my data</Ui> on the Rules tab, a card headed "Tune "Busy nights"?" may propose moving an occupancy threshold, for example from 80% to 95%. <Ui>Make that change</Ui> is an edit of that one number: the rule keeps its name, its other settings and its place in the list. If the rule is on, the same window opens first, with Apply and Skip. Only occupancy thresholds are offered this way, and only when yours is more than 10 percentage points away from what your own history supports. A rule named "Filling-up raise" or "Nearly-full raise", as the starter rules built from your own bookings are, never gets one, since its threshold came from those bookings. Read [Getting suggestions later](/docs/review/getting-suggestions-later).

## Starting again instead

To replace a rule rather than change it, delete it (<Ui>Delete Rule and Undo Its Changes</Ui> undoes every price change it made) and add a new one, or switch it off and add the new one beside it. A new rule starts with its <Ui>Fired</Ui> count at a dash, and none of the old rule's "Stop for this night" answers. Read [Off, delete or change](/docs/rules/off-delete-or-change).

<Callout kind="not-yet">
There is no copy or duplicate, and no way to copy a rule to another property. Add the rule again there.
</Callout>
