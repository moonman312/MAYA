<InPlainWords>
A rule fires only when every one of its conditions is true on the room types it measures, and only where nothing holds it back: a wait, a stronger rule, a ceiling or floor already reached, or history it does not have. The day card shows the whole property, while a rule reads only its own room types, and <Ui>Greater than</Ui> 80 means more than 80, not 80 exactly. The <Ui>Fired</Ui> column on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab tells you whether it has ever acted.
</InPlainWords>

## The day card says 85% but my 80% rule did not fire

Two things to check.

**Which room types the rule measures.** The day card's occupancy (its big percentage, unless your property picked another number in Settings) is sellable occupancy over every room type ticked as a room. A rule reads occupancy over the room types in its own list. If a rule such as "Busy Standard" measures only Standard, and Standard is 75% full while Deluxe and Suite are full, the property reads 85% and the rule reads 75%. The <Ui>Room Types</Ui> column on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab shows the list, and "Watches Standard · Changes Suite" when the rule measures one set and changes another.

**The comparison is strict.** <Ui>Greater than</Ui> 80 fires at 80.01% and not at 80.00%. <Ui>Less than</Ui> 50 fires at 49.9% and not at 50.00%. To fire at exactly 80% as well, set the threshold to 79.99 (the box takes decimals).

Also check the rule's other conditions. A rule with occupancy and a booking window needs both true at once. "Busy nights" with a booking window <Ui>Less than</Ui> 7 added does nothing on a night 20 days out, however full it is.

## The rule looks true but nothing happened

Work down this list. Most are visible on screen.

1. **The rule is Off.** The <Ui>Status</Ui> switch on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab. An Off rule is skipped on every night.
2. **It measures nothing.** Only room types ticked as <Ui>Counts as a room</Ui> are measured. A rule whose measured list holds only a meeting room, a court or a spa slot reads nothing, so its conditions can never be true. A room type switched off in MAYA (an empty duplicate hidden during the review, or one we switched off after you removed it from your system) is dropped from the list too.
3. **It changes nothing.** A rule needs at least one room type to change, and the builder refuses to save without one. A room type switched off in MAYA is never priced, so a rule that changes only that type does nothing. A room type you only removed in your system stays priced until we switch it off, but nights that enter the 396-night window after you removed it have no rate to start from, so nothing fires on them (item 10).
4. **It is waiting.** A booking speed or pickup rule leaves a night alone for its wait after acting on it, from 1 day to 2 weeks, per night and room type. A price you type on the night restarts the wait from that moment for every rule that existed when you typed it.
5. **It counts only what came in after the latest raise.** Once a night has been raised, a booking speed rule that raises counts from the newest raise still on that night by itself or a stronger rule. A weaker rule's raise never moves where it counts from. On the day of the raise, only the bookings that arrived after it count. A rule that raises on a fast pace needs those alone to beat what similar nights get in its whole window; any other rule compares them with similar nights over the same days. Cuts work the same way among the rules that cut, in full days up to yesterday. A raise by a rule with only occupancy or booking window conditions never moves where counting starts. After a price you type, the rule reads its whole window again.
6. **A stronger rule holds the night.** Only one booking speed or pickup rule fires on a night and room type in a run. A waiting rule whose condition still holds keeps the night, so nothing weaker fires under it. When two are ready at once, higher priority wins first (starter rules before yours, and every rule you build has the same priority). Then the one with more conditions wins, then, for two pickup rules with the same <Ui>Compare</Ui>, the stricter threshold, then the larger money move, then the older rule. Read [which one fires when several match](/docs/rules/how-rules-run#which-one-fires-when-several-match).
7. **The price is already at the limit.** A raise does not fire on a night already at its ceiling. A cut does not fire on a night at its floor. Neither counts as a fire.
8. **Not enough history.** A booking speed rule needs similar past nights, or failing those enough nearby nights to read momentum from. Momentum is how nearby nights are booking now compared with the same nights a year earlier. With neither it stays quiet. The change log's <Ui>Show the numbers</Ui> says "Booking speed wasn't rated for this night, so rules that watch booking speed left it alone."
9. **No pickup reading from the window's start.** A pickup count rule compares booked rooms now with booked rooms when its lookback window opened (1, 3 or 7 days ago). MAYA needs a reading from that moment for every room type the rule measures. A property that connected two days ago has no reading from 3 days ago, so a 3-day pickup rule stays quiet until it does, and a room type ticked as a room today has none either. A reading more than 12 hours older than the window's start does not count.
10. **The night has no starting price.** A night your system has at 0, or has no rate for at all, with no price typed in MAYA, is not priced, so nothing can fire on it. A booking's rate is never a starting price.
11. **A standard rule is already holding.** A rule with only occupancy or booking window conditions fires once when its condition starts to hold, then holds. It does not fire again while the condition stays true, and the <Ui>Fired</Ui> count does not climb.
12. **The night is outside the window.** Your rules price tonight and the next 395 nights. Past nights are never touched.

<Callout kind="good-to-know" title="The Fired column">
<Ui>Fired</Ui> shows how many times a rule has acted, once per night and room type, including changes that later came off. A dash means never. Its <Ui>?</Ui> explains the count.
</Callout>

## It fired on Deluxe but not Standard

Rules act per room type, so every hold in the list above applies room type by room type.

- **Different ceilings and floors.** Standard's $300 ceiling may already be reached while Deluxe has room under its $400.
- **Different waits.** Each room type waits on its own. Standard may still be inside its wait from an earlier fire while Deluxe is free.
- **Different counts.** One rule reads the same count for every room type it changes, taken over the room types it measures. A booking speed rule's count differs between two room types only when the newest change it counts from, by itself or a stronger rule, was made at a different time on each, or you typed a price on one of them after that, which sends the rule back to its whole window there. To read each room type on its own, make one rule per room type, each measuring and changing only itself.
- **A stronger rule holds Standard.** Another rule may be waiting on Standard with its condition still true, which keeps weaker rules off that room type.

The change log lists each room type's change separately, so you can read the reason for the one that fired and look for the hold on the one that did not.

## It fired 395 days out but not 397

Your rules price a window of 396 nights: tonight and the next 395, counted on your property's own calendar. A night 397 days out is not priced or sent, and no rule reads it. It enters the window the day after tomorrow, and rules act on it then.

You can type a price up to 399 days ahead. It is kept, and the night is priced and sent once it enters the window.

## A rule that measures only the pickleball court never fires

Only room types ticked as <Ui>Counts as a room</Ui> are measured. Occupancy is rooms booked over rooms you can sell, and booking speed counts bookings, and neither is worked out for a court, a meeting room or a spa slot. A rule whose measured list holds only such types measures nothing and never fires.

It can still price the court. Tick <Ui>Change prices on different room types</Ui> in the builder, put a real room type under <Ui>Measure</Ui> and the court under <Ui>Change</Ui>. The rule then reads the room type's occupancy or booking speed and changes the court's price. Read [Measure one set of room types, change another](/docs/rules/measure-one-set-change-another).

When a rule measures a mix, MAYA drops the non-rooms from the figure and says so in the change log: "That 95% leaves out Pickleball Court."

## The Rate Simulator says "Conditions not met" or "No occupancy for its signal room types"

The <Ui>Rate Simulator</Ui> tab lists every rule that did not fire under "Didn't fire", with one of these reasons:

| Reason | What it means |
|---|---|
| "Conditions not met" | The rule's conditions were false for the numbers you typed. The line adds what it saw: "(it saw 70% occupancy, 30 days out)". |
| "No occupancy for its signal room types" | None of the room types the rule measures counts as a room, or you gave them no rooms. |
| "Rule has no room types attached" | The rule has nothing to change. |
| "Outside the rule's date window" | The rule has a date range. Rules you make in the app have none, so you should not see this. |
| "This day of the week is excluded" | The rule runs only on some days. Rules you make in the app run every day. A starter rule whose name ends in "(Fri and Sat)" or "(Sun to Thu)" runs only on those nights, because it copies a move you made only on them. |

The simulator's <Ui>Booking speed</Ui> select starts on "Not enough history", which keeps booking speed rules quiet, exactly as your booking speed rules do on a real night when there is not enough history. Pick a level to see what they would do.

The simulator differs from a real run in three ways it states on screen: a standard rule shows where the night settles rather than each step, every matching booking speed rule is added rather than one winner chosen, and a rule that would fire again after its wait is shown once. Read [The Rate Simulator](/docs/rules/the-rate-simulator).

## The rule changed the wrong room type

The room type list in the builder does two jobs. Unticked, <Ui>Apply to room types</Ui> is both the set the rule measures and the set it changes. Ticked, <Ui>Change prices on different room types</Ui> splits it into <Ui>Measure</Ui> and <Ui>Change</Ui>. The rule card reads "Watches Standard, Deluxe · Changes Suite" when the two differ.

If a rule changed a room type you did not expect, it is in that rule's <Ui>Change</Ui> list. Click <Ui>Edit</Ui> on the rule, untick it and click <Ui>Save changes</Ui>. With <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui>, the rule's changes come off that room type from the next run. Read [Change a rule](/docs/recipes/change-a-rule).

## A Viewer's rule did not save

Viewers can look but not change. A Viewer who presses <Ui>Add Rule</Ui> or <Ui>Save changes</Ui>, flips a switch or presses <Ui>Delete</Ui> sees "Only a Revenue Manager or above can change this." in red, under the form or under the rule, and nothing is saved. Rules need Revenue Manager access or higher. Ask a General Manager or Hotel Admin to change your role on the <Ui>Team</Ui> page. Read [Roles](/docs/team/roles).

## A rule I turned off still affects what another rule counts

Turning a rule <Ui>Off</Ui> freezes what it has done: its changes stay on the price. A change made by a booking speed or pickup rule that is Off also still marks where weaker rules start counting, because the bookings behind it were already acted on. Once a night carries a change, a booking speed or pickup rule counts only bookings made since the newest change still on that night by itself or a stronger rule moving the price the same way, switched-off rules included. Delete the rule if you want its changes and its marks gone.

<Example title='Why "Busy Standard" stayed quiet at The Harbour Inn'>
Friday reads 85% on the day card: Standard 9 of 12, Deluxe 6 of 6, Suite 2 of 2. Priya's rule "Busy Standard" (occupancy Greater than 80, raise 10%) measures and changes Standard only. Standard is 9 of 12, which is 75%, under the 80 mark. The rule is right to stay quiet. Sam changes nothing. On Saturday Standard reaches 10 of 12, which is 83.3%, and the rule fires: "Busy Standard" raised this night 10%, from $150.00 to $165.00. It was 83% full, past the 80% mark you set.
</Example>
