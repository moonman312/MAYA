<InPlainWords>
Start in the <Ui>Change Log</Ui> tab: every change names the rule and the reason. A big jump is usually two rules stacked (percentages compound). A $1 or $99,999 price is the default floor or ceiling, which stop nothing in most currencies, so set real ones. A change with no rule named is your own rate changing in your property system, or a price someone typed. A night with no price is past the 396-night window (tonight and the next 395), closed in your system, or not run yet.
</InPlainWords>

## Why did the price jump 50%?

Two rules acted on the same night, and percentages compound. Standard rules (occupancy and booking window) apply first, then each booking speed or pickup fire in the order it was made, then the floor and ceiling. A +10% followed by a +25% is not +35%: $200 becomes $220, then $275.

The change log shows the chain with running prices, each step followed by its own reason:

> "Busy nights" raised this night 10%, from $200.00 to $220.00.
> It was 85% full, past the 80% mark you set.
> Then "Hot-week surge" raised it 25%, from $220.00 to $275.00.
> Bookings came in much faster than normal this past week: 12, against the 5 a night like this usually has by now.

Once three of one booking speed rule's changes are on the same night, the amber <Ui>Rules that keep adjusting</Ui> banner asks you whether to carry on or stop. Until you answer, it keeps going. Read [Rules that keep adjusting](/docs/rules/rules-that-keep-adjusting).

If the jump is bigger than you want, set a ceiling. The default ceiling is $99,999.99, which stops nothing in most currencies. Read [Floors and ceilings](/docs/review/floors-and-ceilings).

<Example title="Two raises at The Harbour Inn">
Deluxe is $200. "Busy nights" (occupancy Greater than 80, Increase 10%) takes it to $220. "Hot-week surge" (Much Faster Than Normal, Increase 25%) takes it to $275. The Deluxe ceiling is $400, so $275 is published. Had the ceiling been $250, the log would add "That would have gone past your $250.00 ceiling for Deluxe, so it stopped there."
</Example>

## Why did a rule raise a night that is empty?

Because booking speed compares a night with what similar past nights had received at the same point before arrival, not with how full it is. A night 50 days out is compared with how its similar nights were booking when they were 50 days out. If those nights had received none at all by then, 2 or 3 bookings in a week read Much Faster Than Normal, or Faster Than Normal when there are fewer than 5 similar nights to compare with. If they had averaged a fraction of a booking, 0.4 say, it takes 3, because a difference of less than 2 bookings always reads Normal.

Press <Ui>Show the numbers</Ui> under the change. The panel that opens shows the count, the usual number ("nights like this one usually get almost no bookings over the same stretch") and which nights were compared. "Almost no bookings" covers anything under 1, none at all included. Read [Booking speed reads oddly](/docs/wrong/booking-speed-reads-oddly) for why small counts read the way they do.

To keep raises off empty nights, add <Ui>Sellable occupancy (%)</Ui> <Ui>Greater than</Ui> as a second condition to your raise rules. A rule needs every condition true at once. Click <Ui>Edit</Ui> on the rule, click <Ui>+ Add condition</Ui>, and click <Ui>Save changes</Ui>. The window then shows the nights that change: <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> takes the rule's raises off the nights the new condition rules out, and <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> leaves them and keeps new raises off. An edited starter rule keeps its place above the rules you build.

## Why did it cut a night that is nearly full?

A cut rule with only a booking speed condition does not look at occupancy. It counts bookings made in its window (past day, week or month, in full days up to yesterday) and compares them with similar nights over the same stretch. A night that filled up months ago and received one booking this month reads slower than a night like it usually books, and the cut fires.

A wedding or group booked as one reservation makes this more likely: for booking speed, a booking counts once however many rooms it holds. Twenty rooms in one reservation are 20 rooms for occupancy and one booking for booking speed.

Fix the night by typing a price on the <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab. Read [Start here](/docs/wrong/start-here) for the wedding weekend case in full.

Prevent it by giving cut rules an occupancy condition (<Ui>Less than</Ui> 50, say), so a full night is never cut. Click <Ui>Edit</Ui> on the rule, add the condition and click <Ui>Save changes</Ui>. With <Ui>Apply price adjustments</Ui> the rule's cuts come off the nights the new condition rules out; with <Ui>Skip price adjustments</Ui> they stay, and no new cut lands on a full night. Read [Change a rule](/docs/recipes/change-a-rule).

## We are sold out tonight. Why is the price still changing?

MAYA does not read how many rooms your system still has for sale. A full night is priced like any other: your rules run on it whenever its bookings change and once a day, and once you are live its price is sent whenever it changes (Cloudbeds and ThinkReservations). That is on purpose. A cancellation puts a room back on sale, and it sells at the rate your system has at that moment, which is MAYA's latest price once you are live.

On a sold-out night:

- **An occupancy raise stays.** A rule such as "Busy nights" keeps its one raise while the night stays above its mark, and, with its undo box ticked, takes it off if cancellations bring the night back down to the mark or under.
- **A booking speed cut can still fire.** A full night takes no new bookings, so its recent count can read slow against similar nights that were still selling at the same point (the question above).
- **Raises already made stay on the price**, as they do on any night.

To keep cuts off full nights, add a <Ui>Sellable occupancy (%)</Ui> row set to <Ui>Less than</Ui> (70, say) to each cut rule: <Ui>Edit</Ui> on its row, <Ui>+ Add condition</Ui>, <Ui>Save changes</Ui>. For an exact price on a sold-out night, type it on the <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab.

Editing a starter rule keeps the rest of it exactly as it is, its rank above rules you built included, and "Slow-date trim" keeps its exactly <Ui>Slower Than Normal</Ui> unless you pick another speed. Read [Change a rule](/docs/recipes/change-a-rule).

## Why is a price at $1?

That is the default floor, $1.00, and it stops nothing in practice. A cut rule that fires again after each wait, on a night that keeps reading slow, walks the price down until the floor catches it. With no floor set, the floor is $1.

Set a floor for each room type. On the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab press <Ui>Get suggestions from my data</Ui>. MAYA offers a floor only for a room type whose floor is still the $1.00 default. It also needs your answer to the floor question during setup, or at least 30 sold nights for that room type. Accept the amount, or type your own, then press <Ui>Set it to</Ui>. On the amber banner, <Ui>Ask MAYA for a floor</Ui> takes you to the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab for the same button.

There is no screen yet for changing one room type's floor once it is set. Answering the floor question from setup again writes the new floor to every room type that counts as a room at once, except one whose ceiling is lower than it. Read [Set or change a floor or ceiling](/docs/recipes/set-a-floor-or-ceiling).

Once a floor is set, a cut rule stops firing on a night that is already at the floor, because it cannot move the price in its own direction. The price stays at the floor until you type a price, delete the rule, or bookings change what the rules read.

## Why is a price at $99,999?

That is the default ceiling, $99,999.99. In most currencies it has the same effect as no ceiling at all. Raise rules that keep firing on a night that keeps reading fast climb until the ceiling stops them.

In a currency where rooms sell for more than 99,999.99 (yen, won, rupiah or dong, for example), a price stuck at 99,999.99 is the default ceiling holding it down, even below your own rate. Set a real ceiling for that room type before you go live. Read [Floors and ceilings](/docs/review/floors-and-ceilings#when-your-rates-are-above-the-default-ceiling).

Set a ceiling the same way as a floor: <Ui>Get suggestions from my data</Ui> on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab, which <Ui>Ask MAYA for a ceiling</Ui> on the amber banner also takes you to. MAYA offers a ceiling only for a room type whose ceiling is still the $99,999.99 default. It also needs your answer to the ceiling question during setup, or at least 200 sold nights for that room type. The number is your answer or one built from the highest rates you have sold at, and you can type your own before pressing <Ui>Set it to</Ui>. As with a floor, there is no screen yet for changing a ceiling once it is set.

<Callout kind="good-to-know" title="The banner tells you when a limit is still the default">
When three of a rule's changes are on a night and the limit is still the default, the banner's night card says so: "Your ceiling for Suite is still MAYA's $99,999.99 default, so the price can climb that far."
</Callout>

## Why is my price stuck at the floor?

Every published price is held between the room type's floor and ceiling. When the rules ask for less than the floor, the floor is published and the change log says "That would have dropped under your $80.00 floor for Standard, so it stopped there."

While the price sits at the floor, a cut rule does not fire again (it cannot move the price down), and a standard rule's cut holds while its condition holds. The price stays at the floor until something changes what the rules read, or until you act.

To lift it: type the price you want on the <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab, or delete the cutting rule (which undoes its changes). Turning the rule <Ui>Off</Ui> keeps its changes, so the price stays at the floor. A typed price below the floor is refused rather than moved: "Below this room type's floor of $80.00." The one exception is 0, which is a comp night.

## Why did a price change with no rule named?

Three shapes of entry have no rule name.

**"The rate moved from $200.00 to $210.00."** MAYA had nothing else to say: no rule fired, no rule came off, no limit stepped in. Nearly always your own rate changed in your property system. MAYA reads your rates about once an hour, and again when your property's date changes, and prices from that base. On a night MAYA has never sent to, a rate you change in Cloudbeds or ThinkReservations becomes the new starting point. Read [Rates changed in your property system](/docs/watch/rates-changed-in-your-pms).

Mews: MAYA reads no rates from Mews, so a night's starting price is the rate of its newest booking (or the last one saved). On a Mews property this line usually means a new booking came in at a different rate.

**"Sam set the base rate to $180.00."** A person typed a price on the <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab. The entry is labelled "Manual price". "A manager" appears when the name is not on file.

**"The base rate was changed in Cloudbeds to $180.00."** On a live property, a rate you changed in your system on a night MAYA had already sent to, an hour or more after the send, is kept as a manual price. The day card shows "Changed in Cloudbeds · $180.00". No name is attached.

A fourth shape names a rule you cannot find: **"Pricing rule"**. That is a rule someone has deleted. The history stays; the name does not.

## Why did the price go back to the base rate?

Four reasons, and the change log names each.

- **A standard rule switched off.** A rule with only occupancy or booking window conditions holds its change while the condition holds. With its undo box ticked, a cancellation that drops occupancy under the mark stops the condition holding, and the change comes off on the next run.
- **A change came off after cancellations.** "Cancellations meant \"Warm-date bump\" was no longer true, so its 10% raise came off." With a rule's undo box ticked, a booking speed or pickup change comes off when bookings it counted cancel and the rule is no longer true for the night, unless bookings made since keep it true. A cut made because a night was booking slowly never comes off this way, and nor does any change from a rule with its undo box unticked.
- **A rule was deleted.** Deleting undoes every change the rule made, from the next run.
- **A manual price was cleared.** <Ui>Clear</Ui> on the room type card hands the night back to your rules. Standard rules (occupancy and booking window) that the typed price had paused apply again at once, if their condition still holds. Booking speed and pickup changes it took off stay off. Those rules change the night again only by firing anew, which they are free to do from the next run.

Read [When a change comes off](/docs/rules/when-a-change-comes-off).

## Why is a night unpriced?

The room type card shows "Current price" followed by a dash when no price has been published for that night and room type. Reasons, most common first:

1. **Beyond 396 nights.** Your rules price tonight and the next 395 nights, so a night further out gets no price. A price you typed further out is kept, and the night is priced and sent once it enters the window.
2. **Closed in your system.** A night whose rate is 0 in Cloudbeds or ThinkReservations, with no price typed in MAYA, is left unpriced. MAYA does not open a closed night at the floor. Type a price if you want the night priced.
3. **Not run yet.** A property that connected minutes ago has no runs until its first cycle. Give it about five minutes.
4. **A past night.** Past nights are never priced or changed.
5. **A room type switched off in MAYA.** A room type hidden during the review (an empty duplicate), or one we switched off after you removed it from your system, is not priced and has no card on the <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab. A room type you only removed in your system stays priced until we switch it off, but nights that enter the 396-night window after you removed it have no rate to start from and show a dash. [Email us](mailto:info@modern-hospitality-solutions.com?subject=Switch%20off%20a%20room%20type) its name and we switch it off. Read [Room types and rooms out of service](/docs/watch/room-types-and-rooms-out-of-service).

Mews: a Mews property has no rate read, so a night's starting price comes from its bookings or from the last saved starting price, and a night with neither stays unpriced.

<Callout kind="not-yet">
The base price a rule started from is not shown on the day card, only the price after rules and limits. The change log shows the starting price in each sentence: "raised this night 10%, from $200.00 to $220.00."
</Callout>

## Why did my rule fire three times and keep going?

Because it keeps going until you answer. Once three of a booking speed or pickup rule's changes are on the same night, the amber <Ui>Rules that keep adjusting</Ui> banner appears at the top of every tab: "\"Hot-week surge\" has 3 raises on Fri, Nov 13 2026." and "It keeps raising these nights until you stop it."

Answer <Ui>Keep adjusting</Ui> and MAYA stops asking about that night. Answer <Ui>Stop for this night</Ui> and the rule makes no more changes there. Only a Revenue Manager or above can answer. Read [Rules that keep adjusting](/docs/rules/rules-that-keep-adjusting).

## I answered Stop but the price did not go back

Stop means no more changes from that rule on that night. What it already did stays: "What it already changed there stays, unless cancellations mean the rule is no longer true." For a rule with its undo box unticked: "What it already changed there stays."

To put the price where you want it, type it on the <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab. To undo everything the rule did on every night, delete the rule. Read [Undo a price change](/docs/recipes/undo-a-price-change).

## Why does the same rule appear twice on one night?

"\"Slow-date trim\" lowered this night 7%, from $200.00 to $186.00." and, after its reason, "Then \"Slow-date trim\" lowered it another 7%, from $186.00 to $172.98." is one rule that fired, waited its wait, and fired again. A booking speed rule can act on the same night more than once. After the first change it counts only bookings made in the full days since the newest cut still on the night by itself or a stronger rule, up to yesterday, compared with similar nights over the same days, and if those still read slow it cuts again. Three of its changes on one night bring the amber banner.

## Why did my typed price disappear?

Three things clear a manual price.

- **Someone pressed <Ui>Clear</Ui>.** The confirmation reads "Cleared. Your rules price this night again." Any Revenue Manager or above on the property can do it.
- **Your system reported the night at 0.** On a live property, a night MAYA has sent to that your system now has at 0 is read as you closing it. The night is left unpriced and the manual price there is cleared. When you load a rate again, MAYA reads it as the starting point.
- **The night passed.** Manual prices apply to tonight onwards.
