<InPlainWords>
The <Ui>Change Log</Ui> tab covers your 10 most recent pricing runs, about the last 50 minutes, newest first. It lists a night when a rule is on its price, someone set its price, or its price differs from its starting point (the price before any rule). Each listed night shows its starting price and new price, then plain sentences saying what each rule on it did and why. A price that goes back to its starting point, with nothing left on it, is not listed.
</InPlainWords>

## What a pricing run is

A pricing run is one pass of MAYA over your property: it reads your bookings, runs every rule that is on against every night in the window (tonight and the next 59 nights), and publishes a new price wherever one changed. When you are live, the changed prices are then sent to your property system. In simulation the run happens in full and the log fills in the same way, but nothing is sent.

Runs happen:

- **About every five minutes**, each time MAYA's scheduled read of your bookings succeeds. A read that fails prices nothing that time, and a property whose subscription has lapsed is skipped.
- **Straight after you save or clear a manual price**, as far as the last night you saved.
- **Straight after you change a room type** (ticking or unticking <Ui>Counts as a room</Ui>, adding or clearing rooms out of service).

A run that finds nothing to change still leaves a mark in the log. That is what <Ui>Show All Cycles</Ui> reveals.

## The two views

The button in the top right toggles between the two views:

- <Ui>Show All Cycles</Ui> is what the button says while you are in the default view. That view keeps runs that list at least one night, every "Rates not reaching" problem, and every answer you gave to a rule that kept adjusting.
- <Ui>Show Changes Only</Ui> is what the button says once you have opened the full view. The full view adds the quiet runs, each reading "Prices checked" followed by a note that nothing needed to change. A run whose only price moves are ones the log does not list reads the same way (see "Moves the log does not list" below).

When none of the 10 runs lists a night, the default view shows no runs at all, and no message says why. Any "Rates not reaching" problem and any answer you gave still show. Click <Ui>Show All Cycles</Ui> to see the quiet runs.

Under the heading sits the <Ui>Your corrections</Ui> panel, closed by default. It lists the past nights you have set aside as not a fair comparison. [Setting a date aside](/docs/watch/not-a-fair-comparison) explains it.

If the log cannot load you see "Couldn't load your price history." with a <Ui>Try again</Ui> link. Whatever had loaded before stays on screen.

## A run entry

Each run is a box. The first line reads:

"Pricing run · Fri, Oct 2, 2026, 3:05 PM PDT (4 min ago)"

The time is shown in your browser's time zone, with the zone's abbreviation, so two people in different places see different clock times for the same run. Hover the line and the exact moment shows as a raw timestamp in UTC, the world reference time, such as 2026-10-02T22:05:12.345+00:00 (3:05 PM in US Pacific time). The part in brackets says how long ago: "just now", "4 min ago", "2 hr ago", "yesterday", "3 days ago", up to 6 days. Older runs carry no bracket.

Under the first line comes either the list of changes or, for a quiet run, the "Prices checked" line.

## A change, line by line

Each change starts with a bold line:

"Standard · stay 2026-11-13: $150.00 up to $165.00 (+10%)"

That is the room type, the stay night, the night's starting point, the price the run published, and the whole move between those two as a percentage. A cut reads "down to" with a negative percentage.

The starting point is the night's price before any rule: your own rate for that night (on Mews, the rate on the newest booking), or a price someone set. It is not the price the night had before this run. So when a second rule stacks on a night an earlier run already raised from $150.00 to $165.00, the bold line still starts from $150.00:

"Standard · stay 2026-11-13: $150.00 up to $206.25 (+37.5%)"

The +37.5% is both raises together, not only this run's step. The sentences under the line show each step on its own. [Rates you change in your property system](/docs/watch/rates-changed-in-your-pms) explains where the starting point comes from.

Then come the sentences, always in this order:

1. **What came off.** If the run stopped applying an earlier change, that comes first: "Warm-date bump" stopped applying an earlier 10% raise here: enough of the bookings behind it cancelled. This line shows only while something else is still on the price: another rule, your floor or ceiling, or a price someone set. When the change that came off was the last thing on the price, the night goes back to its starting point and gets no entry at all.
2. **Each rule on the price, then why.** Every rule still applying to the night is listed, not only the one that acted in this run. The first reads "Warm-date bump" raised this night 10%, from $150.00 to $165.00. A second rule on the same night reads Then "Hot-week surge" raised it 25%, from $165.00 to $206.25. The "from" price of each later rule is the price the earlier one left, which is how stacking shows itself.
3. **The limit, if one stepped in.** That would have gone past your $300.00 ceiling for Standard, so it stopped there.
4. **A fallback** when there is nothing else to say: The rate moved from $150.00 to $160.00.

Only one booking speed or pickup rule can fire on a night for one room type in one run. So when two are listed, the first fired on an earlier run and still applies. Its reason then carries no numbers, because those were read on the run it fired.

Under the sentences, a change carries a <Ui>How did we know?</Ui> button when the run read booking speed for that night. With a booking speed rule that measures the whole property (every starter rule does), that is nearly every change, whichever rule made it, typed prices included. [The numbers behind a change](/docs/watch/how-did-we-know) covers what it opens.

## The sentences, by kind of rule

Every "why" sentence is built from your own rule: the mark you typed, the room types it measures, and the numbers the run saw. The shapes below use The Harbour Inn's rules.

### Occupancy and booking window

These two share one sentence, because you set them as one condition.

| Rule | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Occupancy Greater than 80 | It was 85% full, past the 80% mark you set. |
| Occupancy Less than 50 | It was 40% full, under the 50% mark you set. |
| Booking window Less than 3 | It had 2 days to go, past the 3-day mark you set. |
| Booking window Greater than 21 | It had 30 days to go, beyond the 21-day mark you set. |
| Both, "Last minute" | It was 40% full with 2 days to go, under the 50% mark and past the 3-day mark you set. |
| Measuring other room types than it changes | Standard and Deluxe were 83% full, past the 80% mark you set. |
| No numbers recorded | This night was past the 80% mark you set. |

Read "past" as "on the far side of the mark in the direction you asked for". A booking window counts down, so a night 2 days out is past a 3-day mark, not short of it.

### Pickup count

"9 bookings arrived in the last 3 days, past the 4-booking mark you set." A rule set to Less than reads "under the 4-booking mark". When the rule measures other room types than the ones it changes, it names them: "9 Standard bookings arrived in the last 3 days, ...". Without a count: "Bookings in the last 3 days came in past the 4-booking mark you set."

For a pickup rule, a room night counts as one, so a 3-room booking for one night reads as 3 here. [Pickup count](/docs/rules/pickup-count) has the detail.

A pickup rule with <Ui>Pickup measures</Ui> set to <Ui>Revenue (currency)</Ui> fires on the money booked, but its sentence still shows the room nights and calls your amount a booking mark. A rule set at 500 reads "9 bookings arrived in the last 3 days, past the 500-booking mark you set." The 500 is the amount you typed, in your property's currency, and the 9 is the room nights gained in those days.

### Booking speed

The sentence opens with the level the rule is written for, then the count, then what a night like this usually has:

| Level in the rule | Opening words |
|---|---|
| Stalled | Bookings all but stopped this past week |
| Surging | Bookings surged this past week |
| Normal | Bookings came in at the normal pace this past week |
| Any other level | The level in lower case: "Bookings came in faster than normal", "Bookings came in much slower than normal", and so on |

"this past week" becomes "this past day" or "this past month" to match the rule's <Ui>Measured over</Ui> setting. Then:

"Bookings came in faster than normal this past week: 9, against the 5 a night like this usually has by now."

The count can read "none" or, after cancellations, "more cancelled than booked". When similar nights usually have fewer than 1 booking by that point it reads "where a night like this usually has almost none by now".

**After the night has already been raised or cut.** A rule that raises never re-counts bookings an earlier raise already acted on. Once a booking speed or pickup rule has raised a night, a booking speed rule that raises counts only the bookings that came in after that raise. One that cuts counts from the night's last cut the same way. The day of the raise or cut is the first day counted, and the sentence names those days:

"Bookings came in faster than normal in the 4 days since this night was last raised: 6, against the 2 a night like this usually gets in those days."

When only the day of the raise or cut has been counted so far, it reads "later on the day this night was last raised" (or "last cut") and "usually gets in a day". [How rules run](/docs/rules/how-rules-run) explains why the count restarts.

When the rule measures other room types than the ones it changes, the subject changes: "Standard and Deluxe bookings came in faster than normal this past week: ...". A rule that measures and changes the same room types reads "Bookings", with no names, even when those are only some of your room types. <Ui>How did we know?</Ui> names the room types whenever a rule measures only some of them.

### A room type left out

When an occupancy rule measured room types that do not count as rooms, those were left out of the figure, and one more sentence says so: "That 82% leaves out Meeting Room."

### The same rule twice on one night

A booking speed or pickup rule can act on the same night again after its wait. The second time reads Then "Hot-week surge" raised it another 25%, from $206.25 to $257.81. The repeat only carries a "why" sentence when the run has fresh numbers for it.

<Callout kind="example" title="Two rules and a ceiling on one night">
Deluxe on Friday, November 13, 2026 starts at $200. "Warm-date bump" raises it 10% to $220. "Hot-week surge" then raises it 25% to $275. The Deluxe ceiling is $400, so $275 is published. Had the ceiling been $250, the log would add: That would have gone past your $250.00 ceiling for Deluxe, so it stopped there.
</Callout>

## Prices people set

A manual price shows as its own change, led by a sentence naming who typed it:

"Priya set the base rate to $180.00."

The name is the person's profile name in MAYA. If no name is stored it reads "A manager". A rate you changed in your property system on a night MAYA had sent to names no person: "The base rate was changed in Cloudbeds to $180.00." (or "in Think Reservations").

With nothing stacked on top, that one sentence is the whole entry, and the bold line reads "$180.00 up to $180.00 (+0%)". If a rule later fires on top of your price, its sentences follow the lead sentence in the usual way.

Deleting a rule removes it for good, and older entries lose its reasons. An occupancy or booking window rule's move still shows, as "Pricing rule" raised (or lowered) this night, with no sentence saying why. A booking speed or pickup rule's move is left out of the entry altogether, so the prices in the remaining sentences may not add up to the bold line. A "stopped applying" line for a deleted rule reads "Pricing rule" too.

## Your answers

When you answer the <Ui>Rules that keep adjusting</Ui> banner, the answer is logged as its own item headed "Your answer · Fri, Oct 2, 2026, 3:12 PM PDT". One item covers every night the answer settled:

- Priya stopped "Hot-week surge" on 4 nights. The raises it already made stay, unless enough of the bookings behind them cancel.
- Priya stopped "Slow-date trim" on Fri, Nov 13 2026. What it already cut stays.
- Priya told "Hot-week surge" to carry on with 4 nights.
- Priya let "Hot-week surge" run again on 4 nights. It can start adjusting again from the next pricing run.

A deleted rule reads "A rule" here. At most 20 answers are shown, within the span of the runs on screen. [Rules that keep adjusting](/docs/rules/rules-that-keep-adjusting) explains the banner and the choices.

## Rates not reaching your property system

When you are live and a price cannot be delivered, the log carries an amber item headed "Rates not reaching Cloudbeds" (or "Think Reservations"). How soon it appears depends on the cause:

- **A problem that needs you**, such as lost access or a price your system refuses: at once.
- **Your system cannot find the rate MAYA writes to**: after one more try. MAYA reads your list of rates again and sends once more on the next run, about five minutes later. If the rate is still missing, the item appears then.
- **A problem that often clears on its own**, such as your system not answering, asking MAYA to slow down or not confirming a send: only once a night has failed at least 5 times over at least 2 hours.

A few holds MAYA applies itself never appear here.

While the problem lasts it sits at the very top, above every run. Once it ends it drops to where it ended, and only stays while that moment is within the runs shown. Mews never has these items, because nothing is sent to Mews.

The item names the cause in one sentence, counts the nights and room types affected, says "Still happening" or when it was resolved, and tells you what to do when there is something to do. <Ui>Show 12 tries</Ui> opens the attempts behind it. [When your property system does not accept a price](/docs/live/when-your-pms-does-not-accept-a-price) walks through every cause.

## How much the log shows

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Runs on screen | The 10 most recent, about the last 50 minutes. A quiet run counts as one of the 10. |
| Changes per run | The 40 biggest moves, largest first, each measured as the whole move from the night's starting point. A run with more changes shows 40 and nothing says how many more there were. |
| What counts as a change | A night whose price differs from its starting point by at least one cent, has a rule still on it, or has a price someone set. |
| Problems and answers | Up to 20 of each, within the span of the runs shown. |
| Detail behind a change | Kept for 90 days. That is how long <Ui>How did we know?</Ui> can open for a change. |

Runs happen about every five minutes, so 10 runs reach back about 50 minutes. Saving a price or changing a room type adds a run, so on a busy day the log reaches back less far. Older runs are not lost the moment they leave the screen, but there is no way to page back to them in the app.

## Moves the log does not list

The log lists a night only when, after the run, its price differs from its starting point, a rule is still on it, or someone set its price. Two kinds of real price move meet none of these, so they get no entry:

- **A price going back to its starting point.** The last thing on the price comes off. The only rule on the night stops holding (for "Busy nights", the night falls to 80% full or less), the bookings behind its only raise cancel, or you clear a price you set on a night no rule is on.
- **A price that moves only because its starting point moved**, on a night no rule is on. On Cloudbeds and ThinkReservations, that includes a new rate MAYA reads from your system (about once an hour) on a night it has never sent a price to. On Mews, it is a newer booking with a different rate.

When nothing else in that run is listed, the full view shows the run as "Prices checked" with a note that nothing needed to change, even though a price moved. To see where a night stands now, open the <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab.

## A change that has left the log

The log shows only the 10 most recent runs, about the last 50 minutes. A price that moved more than about an hour ago is no longer on screen, and a change from earlier today has usually left it. MAYA keeps the detail behind every change for 90 days: the night's starting price and the price published, which rules acted, and the numbers each one saw. [Email us](mailto:info@modern-hospitality-solutions.com?subject=Why%20did%20a%20price%20change) with the room type, the night and roughly when the price moved, and we can look the change up for you within those 90 days. After 90 days the detail is gone.

There is no screen that shows one night's price history. The <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab shows each night's current price, with a badge while a price typed in MAYA or changed in your system is in place. It does not show the prices the night had before. To keep your own record, copy the entries you care about while they are still in the log.

## Money and names

The sentences under each change follow your property's currency: "$" for US dollars, "€" for euros, "£" for pounds, and the three-letter code in front of the amount for any other currency. The bold line above them, like the calendar and the simulator, always shows "$". So a property in euros sees "€150.00" in a sentence and "$150.00" in the bold line for the same price.

Everyone on the property can read the change log, whatever their role.
