<InPlainWords>
The <Ui>Change Log</Ui> tab shows your 10 most recent pricing runs that changed a price, newest first, however long ago they ran, and <Ui>Older</Ui> at the bottom adds the 10 before them, back through the 90 days MAYA keeps. Each listed night shows its starting price and new price, a short line saying where that price went, then plain sentences saying what each rule on it did and why. A run made while your property was simulating is tagged <Ui>Simulation</Ui> and says what would have happened, and that nothing was sent. A price going back to its starting point is listed too, with what came off. The runs in between that changed nothing are counted, not listed: <Ui>Show All Cycles</Ui> shows each stretch of them as one line, such as "Prices checked 47 times, nothing needed to change".
</InPlainWords>

## What a pricing run is

A pricing run is one pass of MAYA over your property: it reads your bookings, runs every rule that is on against the nights that need it, and publishes a new price wherever one changed. The nights that need it are every night whose bookings changed since the last run and every night a change of yours reaches, and, in the first runs after midnight at your property, every night in the window (tonight and the next 395 nights), nearest first. A run where nothing needed pricing still counts as a run that changed nothing. When you are live, the changed prices are then sent to your property system. In simulation the run happens in full and the log fills in the same way, but nothing is sent, and the log words each change as what would have happened ([Runs made in simulation](#runs-made-in-simulation)).

Runs happen:

- **About every five minutes**, each time MAYA's scheduled read of your bookings succeeds. A read that fails prices nothing that time, and a property whose subscription has lapsed is skipped.
- **Straight after you save or clear a manual price**, on the nights you saved.
- **Straight after you change a room type** (ticking or unticking <Ui>Counts as a room</Ui>, adding or clearing rooms out of service): the change asks for a run at once. Rooms out of service re-price their own nights. A room type setting re-prices every night in the window over that run and the next few, nearest first.

A run that finds nothing to change is counted into a quiet line between the runs that did. <Ui>Show All Cycles</Ui> shows those lines.

## The two views

The button in the top right toggles between the two views:

- <Ui>Show All Cycles</Ui> is what the button says while you are in the default view. That view shows the runs that changed a price, every "Prices not reaching" problem, every answer you gave to a rule that kept adjusting, every item about rates changed in your property system, and every switch between simulation and live.
- <Ui>Show Changes Only</Ui> is what the button says once you have opened the full view. The full view adds the quiet lines. Each counts a stretch of runs in a row that changed nothing, with when the first and last of them ran, in small grey type: "Prices checked 47 times from Sep 24, 10:05 AM to 1:55 PM (4 min ago), nothing needed to change." A stretch of one reads "Prices checked once at Sep 24, 1:55 PM". The line under the oldest change shown starts "Just before that, prices were checked 12 times".

When no run has changed a price yet, the default view shows no runs at all, and no message says why. Any "Prices not reaching" problem and any answer you gave still show. Click <Ui>Show All Cycles</Ui> to see the quiet lines.

Under the heading sits the <Ui>Your corrections</Ui> panel, closed by default. It lists the past nights you have set aside as not a fair comparison. [Setting a date aside](/docs/watch/not-a-fair-comparison) explains it.

If the log cannot load you see "Couldn't load your price history." with a <Ui>Try again</Ui> link. Whatever had loaded before stays on screen.

## A run entry

Each run is a box. The first line reads:

"Pricing run · Fri, Oct 2, 2026, 3:05 PM PDT (4 min ago)"

A run made while your property was simulating carries a small amber <Ui>Simulation</Ui> tag after that line.

The time is shown in your browser's time zone, with the zone's abbreviation, so two people in different places see different clock times for the same run. Hover the line and the exact moment shows, to the second, on the same clock: "Oct 2, 2026, 3:05:12 PM PDT". The part in brackets says how long ago: "just now", "4 min ago", "2 hr ago", "yesterday", "3 days ago", up to 6 days. Older runs carry no bracket.

Under the first line comes the list of changes, the biggest moves first. A run that made more than 40 changes lists its 40 biggest and ends with a line counting the rest, such as "And 23 more changes in this run." A simulated run's line reads "And 23 more prices would have changed in this run."

## A change, line by line

Each change starts with a bold line:

"Standard · stay 2026-11-13: $150.00 up to $165.00 (+10%)"

That is the room type, the stay night, the night's starting point, the price the run published, and the whole move between those two as a percentage. A cut reads "down to" with a negative percentage. In a simulated run the bold line reads differently: "Simulation: the price for Fri Nov 13, Standard would have gone from $150.00 to $165.00." ([Runs made in simulation](#runs-made-in-simulation)).

Under the bold line, a short line says where the price went: "Sent to Cloudbeds.", "Waiting to be sent to Cloudbeds." and so on, or "Nothing was sent to Cloudbeds." in simulation. [Where the price went](#where-the-price-went) lists them.

The starting point is the night's price before any rule: your own rate for that night, or a price someone set (on Mews, always a price someone set). It is not the price the night had before this run. So when a second rule stacks on a night an earlier run already raised from $150.00 to $165.00, the bold line still starts from $150.00:

"Standard · stay 2026-11-13: $150.00 up to $206.25 (+37.5%)"

The +37.5% is both raises together, not only this run's step. The sentences under the line show each step on its own. [Rates you change in your property system](/docs/watch/rates-changed-in-your-pms) explains where the starting point comes from.

Then come the sentences, always in this order:

1. **What came off.** If cancellations took a change off (the rule's undo box is ticked), that comes first: Cancellations meant "Warm-date bump" was no longer true, so its 10% raise came off. The next sentence gives the numbers the check found, such as: Of the 9 bookings it counted, 4 are still booked, where nights like it usually get about 5, and the rule needs a booking speed of at least faster than normal. A change taken off by a typed price reads "Warm-date bump" stopped applying an earlier 10% raise here: this night's price was set by hand.
2. **Each rule on the price, then why.** Every rule still applying to the night is listed, not only the one that acted in this run. The first reads "Warm-date bump" raised this night 10%, from $150.00 to $165.00. A second rule on the same night reads Then "Hot-week surge" raised it 25%, from $165.00 to $206.25. The "from" price of each later rule is the price the earlier one left, which is how stacking shows itself.
3. **The limit, if one stepped in.** That would have gone past your $300.00 ceiling for Standard, so it stopped there.
4. **A fallback** when there is nothing else to say: The rate moved from $150.00 to $160.00.

When the run put the night back at its starting point, with nothing left on it, the entry names what came off and ends with the move: "That took this night from $165.00 to $150.00." Its bold line starts from the price the night had before, "Standard · stay 2026-11-13: $165.00 down to $150.00 (-9.1%)". A standard rule that switched off reads "Busy nights" stopped applying an earlier 10% raise here: this night no longer met its conditions. A cleared price reads "The price set by hand was cleared." And a night with nothing on it whose starting point moved reads "The base rate for this night changed from $150.00 to $160.00."

Only one booking speed or pickup rule can fire on a night for one room type in one run. So when two are listed, the first fired on an earlier run and still applies. Its reason then carries no numbers, because those were read on the run it fired.

Under the sentences, a change carries a <Ui>Show the numbers</Ui> button when the run read booking speed for that night. With a booking speed rule that measures the whole property (every booking speed starter rule does), that is nearly every change, whichever rule made it, typed prices included. [The numbers behind a change](/docs/watch/how-did-we-know) covers what it opens.

## Runs made in simulation

While your property is in simulation MAYA runs every rule and writes every change here, but nothing is sent, so nothing in the log says a price changed. Each change says what would have happened instead:

- The run's first line carries the amber <Ui>Simulation</Ui> tag.
- The bold line reads "Simulation: the price for Fri Nov 13, Standard would have gone from $150.00 to $165.00." For a price someone typed, with nothing on top of it, it reads "Simulation: the price for Fri Nov 13, Standard would have been $180.00."
- The line under it reads "Nothing was sent to Cloudbeds." (or your system's name, or "your property system" with none connected). Once you are live it reads "Nothing was sent to Cloudbeds at the time."
- Each step says "would have": "Warm-date bump" would have raised this night 10%, from $150.00 to $165.00. Then "Hot-week surge" would have raised it 25%, from $165.00 to $206.25.
- What came off: Cancellations meant "Warm-date bump" was no longer true, so its 10% raise would have come off. "Busy nights" would have stopped applying an earlier 10% raise here: this night no longer met its conditions.
- A limit: That would have gone past your $300.00 ceiling for Standard, so it would have stopped there.
- A night put back at its starting point: That would have taken this night from $165.00 to $150.00. With nothing else to say: The price would have moved from $150.00 to $160.00.
- A price typed in MAYA: "Priya typed a price of $180.00."

What the run saw stays as it was: "It was 82% full, past the 70% mark you set." is true in either mode. So is "The base rate for this night changed from $150.00 to $160.00.", which is your own rate in your system, and "The price set by hand was cleared."

The label follows the moment of the run, not the mode you are in now. Once you go live, the runs from before keep their <Ui>Simulation</Ui> tag and their "would have" words, and new runs read as live.

Once you go live, the first live cycles send every night's price, including prices worked out in simulation ([Going live](/docs/live/going-live)). So a simulated change's line then reads "Nothing was sent to Cloudbeds at the time.", and when its run was the last to price that night and the night still has its price, a second line says what became of it, from MAYA's record of its sends:

| Second line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sent to Cloudbeds after you went live. | That price went out after you went live. |
| Waiting to be sent to Cloudbeds now that you're live. | Not sent yet: the next cycle sends it. |
| Couldn't be sent to Cloudbeds after you went live. | MAYA has stopped trying at this price. |
| Held back after you went live, not sent to Cloudbeds. | MAYA held it back on purpose, or the room type has no rate in your system. |

A simulated change whose price a later run replaced gets no second line, and nor does one on Mews, where nothing is sent.

MAYA keeps a record of every switch between simulation and live, and reads each run's mode from it. For runs from before it kept that record, it works the mode out from what it had on file: a property that had never been live has every run marked as simulated. Where it cannot tell, for example a run from before mid-September 2026 on a property that was already live then, the run reads as a live one always has, with no line about sending.

## Where the price went

On a live property, the line under a change's bold line says what became of its price, as MAYA's own record of its sends shows it:

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sent to Cloudbeds. | The price was sent and your system took it. |
| Waiting to be sent to Cloudbeds. | Not sent yet: the next cycle sends it, it is on its way, or a send is being tried again, including once you clicked <Ui>Try again</Ui> or reconnected after MAYA had stopped trying. |
| Couldn't be sent to Cloudbeds. | MAYA has stopped trying at this price. A "Prices not reaching Cloudbeds" item says why when it needs you ([below](#prices-not-reaching-your-property-system)). |
| Held back, not sent to Cloudbeds. | MAYA held the price back on purpose, or the room type has no rate in your system to send to. |
| Nothing was sent to Mews. MAYA doesn't send prices there yet. | A Mews property, on every live change. |
| Nothing was sent to Cloudbeds. | A run made in simulation ([above](#runs-made-in-simulation)). "at the time" is added once you are live. |

MAYA keeps one record per night and room type: its latest send. So a change gets one of the live lines only when its run was the last to price that night and room type, and the night's price is still its price. That is checked against MAYA's own records, not against what the log has on screen: a later run counts even when it is on a newer page, or its change for that night is one of those only counted under "And 23 more changes in this run." An older change, whose price a later run replaced, has no line at all, because MAYA can no longer say whether that price went. Nor does a night already over that was never sent: nothing will send it now.

"Waiting to be sent" is only said while MAYA can send the price. When your property is back in simulation, MAYA's work is paused over your subscription, the connection reads Disconnected, or the night is beyond the 396-night window, an unsent price gets no line. Everyone on the property sees these lines, whatever their role.

## The sentences, by kind of rule

Every "why" sentence is built from your own rule: the mark you typed, the room types it measures, and the numbers the run saw. The shapes below use The Harbour Inn's rules.

### Occupancy and booking window

These two share one sentence, because you set them as one condition.

| Rule | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Occupancy Greater than 80 | It was 85% full, past the 80% mark you set. |
| Occupancy Less than 50 | It was 40% full, under the 50% mark you set. |
| Booking window Less than 3 | It had 2 days to go, past the 3-day mark you set. |
| Booking window Greater than 21 | It had 30 days to go, beyond the 21-day mark you set. |
| Both, "Last minute" | It was 40% full with 2 days to go, under the 50% mark and past the 3-day mark you set. |
| Measuring other room types than it changes | Standard and Deluxe were 83% full, past the 80% mark you set. |
| No numbers recorded | This night was past the 80% mark you set. |

Read "past" as "on the far side of the mark in the direction you asked for". A booking window counts down, so a night 2 days out is past a 3-day mark, not short of it.

### Pickup count

"9 bookings arrived in the last 3 days, past the 4-booking mark you set." A rule set to Less than reads "under the 4-booking mark". When the rule counted only from the newest change on the night by itself or a stronger rule, the sentence says so instead of naming the window: "6 bookings arrived since this rule or a stronger one's latest raise still on this night, past the 4-booking mark you set." When the rule measures other room types than the ones it changes, it names them: "9 Standard bookings arrived in the last 3 days, ...". Without a count: "Bookings in the last 3 days came in past the 4-booking mark you set."

For a pickup rule, a room night counts as one, so a 3-room booking for one night reads as 3 here. [Pickup count](/docs/rules/pickup-count) has the detail.

A pickup rule with <Ui>Pickup measures</Ui> set to <Ui>Revenue (currency)</Ui> fires on the money booked, but its sentence still shows the room nights and calls your amount a booking mark. A rule set at 500 reads "9 bookings arrived in the last 3 days, past the 500-booking mark you set." The 500 is the amount you typed, in your property's currency, and the 9 is the room nights gained in those days.

### Booking speed

The sentence opens with the level the rule is written for, then the count, then what a night like this usually has:

| Level in the rule | Opening words |
|---|---|
| Stalled | Bookings all but stopped this past week |
| Surging | Bookings surged this past week |
| Normal | Bookings came in at the normal pace this past week |
| Any other level | The level in lower case: "Bookings came in faster than normal", "Bookings came in much slower than normal", and so on |

"this past week" becomes "this past day" or "this past month" to match the rule's <Ui>Measured over</Ui> setting. A rule that cuts counts full days only, so its sentence reads "in the week up to yesterday" and "usually gets in those days". Then:

"Bookings came in faster than normal this past week: 9, against the 5 a night like this usually has by now."

The count can read "none" or, after cancellations, "more cancelled than booked". When similar nights usually have fewer than 1 booking by that point it reads "where a night like this usually has almost none by now".

**After the night has already been raised or cut.** Once a rule has changed a night, it counts only the bookings made since the newest change on that night by itself or by a stronger rule that moves the price the same way. The log cannot say which of those rules made the change, so the sentence names both and the days counted. For a raise, the day of the raise is the first day:

"Bookings came in much faster than normal in the 4 days since this rule or a stronger one's latest raise still on this night: 12, against the 5 a night like this usually gets in a whole week."

"in a whole week" is for a rule that raises on a fast pace: those bookings alone had to beat a whole window of a similar night. Any other rule reads "usually gets in those days". When only the day of the raise has been counted so far, it reads "later on the day of this rule or a stronger one's latest raise still on this night" and, for any other rule, "usually gets in a day". After a cut the count starts on the day after it, in full days: "in the 3 full days after this rule or a stronger one's latest cut still on this night". [How rules run](/docs/rules/how-rules-run#counting-after-a-change) explains why the count restarts.

When the rule measures other room types than the ones it changes, the subject changes: "Standard and Deluxe bookings came in faster than normal this past week: ...". A rule that measures and changes the same room types reads "Bookings", with no names, even when those are only some of your room types. <Ui>Show the numbers</Ui> names the room types whenever a rule measures only some of them.

### A room type left out

When an occupancy rule measured room types that do not count as rooms, those were left out of the figure, and one more sentence says so: "That 82% leaves out Meeting Room."

### The same rule twice on one night

A booking speed or pickup rule can act on the same night again after its wait. The second time reads Then "Hot-week surge" raised it another 25%, from $206.25 to $257.81. The repeat only carries a "why" sentence when the run has fresh numbers for it.

<Callout kind="example" title="Two rules and a ceiling on one night">
Deluxe on Friday, November 13, 2026 starts at $200. "Warm-date bump" raises it 10% to $220. "Hot-week surge" then raises it 25% to $275. The Deluxe ceiling is $400, so $275 is published. Had the ceiling been $250, the log would add: That would have gone past your $250.00 ceiling for Deluxe, so it stopped there.
</Callout>

## Prices people set

A manual price shows as its own change, led by a sentence naming who typed it:

"Priya set the base rate to $180.00."

The name is the person's profile name in MAYA. If no name is stored it reads "A manager". A price our staff typed for you reads "MAYA support set the base rate to $180.00." A rate you changed in your property system on a night MAYA had sent to names no person: "The base rate was changed in Cloudbeds to $180.00." (or "in Think Reservations").

With nothing stacked on top, that one sentence is the whole entry, and the bold line reads "Standard · stay 2026-11-13: $180.00 up to $180.00 (+0%)". If a rule later fires on top of your price, its sentences follow the lead sentence in the usual way.

A price typed while your property is simulating reads "Priya typed a price of $180.00.", under the bold line "Simulation: the price for Fri Nov 13, Standard would have been $180.00." and the line "Nothing was sent to Cloudbeds."

Deleting a rule removes it for good, and older entries lose its reasons. An occupancy or booking window rule's move still shows, as "Pricing rule" raised (or lowered) this night, with no sentence saying why. A booking speed or pickup rule's move is left out of the entry altogether, so the prices in the remaining sentences may not add up to the bold line. A "stopped applying" line for a deleted rule reads "Pricing rule" too.

## Your answers

When you answer the <Ui>Rules that keep adjusting</Ui> banner, the answer is logged as its own item headed "Your answer · Fri, Oct 2, 2026, 3:12 PM PDT". One item covers every night the answer settled:

- Priya stopped "Hot-week surge" on 4 nights. What it already changed stays, unless cancellations mean the rule is no longer true.
- Priya stopped "Slow-date trim" on Fri, Nov 13 2026. What it already changed stays. (A rule with its undo box unticked.)
- Priya stopped "Hot-week surge" on 4 nights. Its simulated changes so far stay, unless cancellations mean the rule is no longer true. (An answer given while your property was simulating: the rule changed no price, so its changes are called simulated.)
- Priya told "Hot-week surge" to carry on with 4 nights.
- Priya let "Hot-week surge" run again on 4 nights. It can start adjusting again from the next pricing run.

A deleted rule reads "A rule" here. At most 20 answers are shown, within the span of the runs on screen. An answer our staff gave for you is headed "MAYA support's answer" instead, and names them as "MAYA support" in the line. [Rules that keep adjusting](/docs/rules/rules-that-keep-adjusting) explains the banner and the choices.

## Going live and back

When someone on your property switches it between simulation and live, the switch is its own item, where it happened among the runs:

- **Went live** · Fri, Oct 2, 2026, 3:12 PM PDT: "Sam took pricing live. From the next cycle, MAYA sends its prices to Cloudbeds."
- **Back to simulation** · ...: "Sam switched pricing back to simulation. MAYA stopped sending prices to Cloudbeds."

It marks where the log's wording changes: runs before a go-live say what would have happened, runs after it say where each price went. It says what going live does, not that a given price went out: each change's own line says that. A person MAYA has no name for reads "A manager". On Mews it reads "Sam took pricing live. MAYA doesn't send prices to Mews yet, so nothing goes to it." A switch our staff made for you shows as a change by MAYA support instead ([below](#changes-by-maya-support)), and the first mode a new property starts in is not an item.

## Changes by MAYA support

When our staff change something on your property for you, each save is logged as its own item headed "Changed by MAYA support · Fri, Oct 2, 2026, 3:12 PM PDT", with one line under it saying what changed:

- Changed by MAYA support: Took pricing live.
- Changed by MAYA support: Changed the pricing rule "Hot-week surge": action_value from 10 to 15.
- Changed by MAYA support: Changed the pricing rule "Hot-week surge": action_value from 10 to 15, 12 days.
- Changed by MAYA support: Removed a team member.

One item per save, however many things it changed. A rule saved together with nights it covers, such as the days it leaves as they are, is one line: the rule's own change, then how many days. A save of nights alone says what kind of thing changed and how many days, as in "Changed a base rate, 2 days." Items sit where they happened among the runs, at most 20 within the span of the runs on screen. Our staff can only make these changes in a time-limited support mode that records every one, as [Can our staff see your property?](/docs/reference/privacy-security-and-your-data#can-our-staff-see-your-property) explains.

## Rates changed in your property system

On Cloudbeds and ThinkReservations, a rate changed or removed in your system on a night MAYA had sent a price to can bring items of its own, headed "Changed in Cloudbeds" (or "Changed in Think Reservations") with the time MAYA read the change. Which ones depends on your setting, in <Ui>Settings</Ui> under your system's name.

With <Ui>MAYA's price wins</Ui>, each night and room type MAYA sends its price to again is one item, naming the night, the room type, your system's rate and MAYA's price. MAYA writes the item when it finds the change, before the next cycle sends its price, so the item says the price went only once MAYA's record of its sends shows it:

- Fri, Nov 13, Standard: changed in Cloudbeds to $175.00. MAYA sent its price, $165.00, again.
- Fri, Nov 13, Standard: the rate was removed in Cloudbeds. MAYA sent its price, $165.00, again.
- Fri, Nov 13, Standard: changed in Cloudbeds to $175.00. MAYA's price, $165.00, is waiting to be sent again. (Until the next cycle sends it.)
- ... MAYA's price, $165.00, couldn't be sent again. (MAYA stopped trying.)
- ... MAYA's price, $165.00, was held back, not sent.
- ... MAYA's price, $165.00, was due to go out again. (MAYA's record no longer says: a later price or a later change has replaced it.)

The warning that something other than MAYA seems to be changing rates comes with <Ui>Keep the change as your price</Ui>, in an amber box: "Something other than MAYA seems to be changing rates in Cloudbeds: 34 rates changed in the last 7 days on nights MAYA had sent a price to. Each change is kept as your price, so MAYA isn't pricing those nights. If you use another pricing tool, turn on "MAYA's price wins"." Its <Ui>Open the setting</Ui> button opens <Ui>Settings</Ui> at your system's section, and is gone once that setting is on.

These items sit where they happened among the runs, within the span of the runs shown. Every warning is listed, and the newest 50 of MAYA's overwrites. One line under them counts the overwrites left out, without checking each one: "And 12 more times MAYA's price was due to go out again over a rate changed in Cloudbeds." A change you keep as your price is not an item of its own: it shows in the next run as "The base rate was changed in Cloudbeds to $180.00." (see [Prices people set](#prices-people-set)). [Rates you change in your property system](/docs/watch/rates-changed-in-your-pms) explains when each happens.

## Prices not reaching your property system

When you are live and a price cannot be delivered, the log carries an amber item headed "Prices not reaching Cloudbeds" (or "Think Reservations"). How soon it appears depends on the cause:

- **A problem that needs you**, such as lost access or a price your system refuses: at once.
- **Your system cannot find the rate MAYA writes to**: after one more try. MAYA reads your list of rates again and sends once more on the next run, about five minutes later. If the rate is still missing, the item appears then.
- **A problem that often clears on its own**, such as your system not answering, asking MAYA to slow down or not confirming a send: only once a night has failed at least 5 times over at least 2 hours.

A few holds MAYA applies itself never appear here.

While the problem lasts it sits at the very top, above every run. Once it ends it drops to where it ended: among the runs shown when that moment is within them, or further down once <Ui>Older</Ui> has added the runs around it. Mews never has these items, because nothing is sent to Mews.

The item names the cause in one sentence, counts the nights and room types affected, says "Still happening" or when it was resolved, and tells you what to do when there is something to do. <Ui>Show 12 tries</Ui> opens the attempts behind it. [When your property system does not accept a price](/docs/live/when-your-pms-does-not-accept-a-price) walks through every cause.

## How far back the log goes

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Runs on screen | The 10 most recent that changed a price, however long ago. Each click of <Ui>Older</Ui> adds the 10 before them. Quiet runs between them are counted into one line per stretch, shown under <Ui>Show All Cycles</Ui>. |
| How far back | 90 days. Runs, and the detail behind them, are kept that long, so the oldest page ends there. |
| Changes per run | The 40 biggest moves, largest first, each measured as the whole move from the night's starting point. A run with more changes shows its 40 biggest, then a line under them such as "And 23 more changes in this run." In a very large run, where more than about 1,000 nights ended at their starting point and not every one was checked for a move back, the count is a minimum and the line reads "And at least 23 more changes in this run." |
| What counts as a change | A night whose price differs from its starting point by at least one cent, has a rule still on it, or has a price someone set, and a night put back at its starting point. |
| Problems and answers | Up to 20 problems still happening, at the top of the first page, and up to 20 that have ended and 20 answers within the span of each page of runs. |
| Rates changed in your system | Every warning, and the newest 50 overwrites within the span of each page of runs, with a line counting the rest. Kept for 180 days. |
| Detail behind a change | Kept for 90 days. That is how long <Ui>Show the numbers</Ui> can open for a change. |

How far back 10 changes reach depends on your property: on a busy day perhaps an hour, on a quiet week several days. <Ui>Older</Ui>, under the last item, adds the next 10 runs that changed a price below them, with the quiet lines between them and everything else that happened in that stretch: problems that ended, your answers, changes by MAYA support and rates changed in your system. A problem still going on stays at the top only. Click it again for the 10 before those, and so on. While a page loads the button reads "Loading…"; if it cannot load, "Couldn't load older history." shows beside it, and clicking it tries again. Once the oldest run kept is on screen the button goes, and "Nothing older. History is kept for 90 days." shows in its place. <Ui>Show Changes Only</Ui> and <Ui>Show All Cycles</Ui> apply to every page shown. Opening the tab again, or switching property, starts again from the newest 10.

## Moves the log does not list

The log lists nearly every price move:

- **A price going back to its starting point** is listed, from the price it had: the only rule on the night stops holding (for "Busy nights", the night falls to 80% full or less), cancellations take its only change off, or you clear a price you set on a night no rule is on. The entry names what came off and ends "That took this night from $165.00 to $150.00."
- **A price that moves only because its starting point moved**, on a night no rule is on, reads "The base rate for this night changed from $150.00 to $160.00." On Cloudbeds and ThinkReservations that includes a new rate MAYA reads from your system (about once an hour). On Mews, it is a newer booking with a different rate.

Two things are left out: moves past the 40 biggest of a run, which are only counted ("And 23 more changes in this run."), and a night arriving in the 396-night window at its starting point with nothing on it. To see where a night stands now, open the <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab.

## Finding an older change

The log opens on the 10 most recent runs that changed a price, so on a busy day a change from this morning may already be below them. Click <Ui>Older</Ui> at the bottom until it shows: each click adds the 10 runs before. MAYA keeps every run and the detail behind each change for 90 days: the night's starting price and the price published, which rules acted, and the numbers each one saw. After 90 days the detail is gone, and the log ends there. If you cannot find a change, [email us](mailto:info@modern-hospitality-solutions.com?subject=Why%20did%20a%20price%20change) with the room type, the night and roughly when the price moved, and we can look it up for you within those 90 days.

To see every time one rule acted, click its count in the <Ui>Fired</Ui> column on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab. Read [The fire log](/docs/rules/off-delete-or-change#the-fire-log).

There is no screen that shows one night's price history. The <Ui>Calendar</Ui> tab shows each night's current price, with a badge while a price typed in MAYA or changed in your system is in place. It does not show the prices the night had before. To keep your own record, copy the entries you care about while they are still in the log.

## Money and names

The bold line and the sentences under each change follow your property's currency: "$" for US dollars, "€" for euros, "£" for pounds, and the three-letter code in front of the amount for any other currency. The calendar and the Rate Simulator use the same sign.

Everyone on the property can read the change log, whatever their role.
