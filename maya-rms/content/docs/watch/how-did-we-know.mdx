<InPlainWords>
Under a change in the change log, click <Ui>How did we know?</Ui> to see the numbers behind it: how many bookings arrived for that night, how many a night like it usually has by then, and the level that reads as. It shows on a change when the run read booking speed for that night. <Ui>Why did you expect that?</Ui> shows how the similar nights were chosen. <Ui>Show me the nights you compared</Ui> lists them one by one, and each one can be set aside if it was not a normal night.
</InPlainWords>

## Where the button appears

<Ui>How did we know?</Ui> sits under a change in the change log, after its sentences. It appears under any change on a night where the run took a booking speed reading. A booking speed rule that measures the whole property (every starter rule does) reads each night it is not waiting on, and that reading goes under every room type's change on the night. So with such a rule on, nearly every change has the button, whichever rule made it: an occupancy, booking window or pickup rule, a price you typed, or a rate changed in your system. The evidence describes how the night was booking, not necessarily the rule that made this change.

The button is missing when the run took no booking speed reading for the night: you have no booking speed rule on, or each one was in its wait on that night. A rule that measures only some room types puts its reading only under the room types it changes.

Anyone on the property can open it, whatever their role. Setting a night aside from inside it needs Revenue Manager access or higher.

Click the button and the panel opens with a short loading line while MAYA fetches what the run recorded. Then you see one block for each window a booking speed rule read on that night, whether that rule acted or not. With the starter rules that is usually four: the past month twice (once up to now for the rules that raise, once in full days up to yesterday for the rules that cut), the past week and the past day. A rule that counted only from a change still on the night adds a block of its own. Each block is read on its own, so their levels can differ.

To find the block behind a booking speed rule's change, read the first sentence of each. Usually it is the block for the rule's <Ui>Measured over</Ui> setting: "In the last 7 days" for a 7-day rule that raises, "In the 7 full days up to yesterday" for one that cuts. But a rule may find the night already raised within that stretch, by itself or a stronger rule, with that raise still on the price. Then it counts only the bookings since, and its block begins "The newest raise still on this night's price". A rule that cuts works the same way after a cut, with "The newest cut still on this night's price".

Say "Warm-date bump" raises a night on a Monday, and "Hot-week surge" raised it the Friday before. The surge changes the price by more, so it is the stronger rule, and the bump counts only the bookings since the surge's raise: its block starts "The newest raise still on this night's price" and covers those 4 days, not 30. The bump's raise never moves where the surge counts from. When the surge raises the night again within 7 days of its own raise, its block starts the same way. Two rules that count from the same raise over the same days show the same numbers, so two blocks can look alike.

If a change turns out to carry no reading, the panel says "No booking-speed observation was recorded for this change." If the fetch fails you see the error and <Ui>Try again</Ui>. <Ui>Close</Ui> folds the panel away.

## The first layer: what arrived, what is usual, and the reading

Three sentences, then sometimes a fourth.

**What arrived.** "In the last 7 days, 9 bookings arrived for this night, with 40 days still to go before arrival." The stretch matches the rule's <Ui>Measured over</Ui> setting. For a rule that cuts it counts full days only, ending yesterday: "In the 7 full days up to yesterday, 9 bookings arrived for this night, ...". When the rule measures some room types only, the count names them: "In the last 7 days, 9 Standard and Deluxe bookings arrived for this night, ...". A booking that holds several rooms counts once.

**What is usual.** "By this point on nights like this one, we would expect about 5 bookings over the same stretch." When similar nights had fewer than 1 booking over that stretch it reads "almost none". The number is what the similar nights had received over the same stretch of their own booking curves, at the same distance before arrival. It is their average, with the highest and lowest left off when there are 5 or more similar nights, and rounded to a whole number wherever it is shown.

**The reading.** "That reads as Faster Than Normal." This is the level the count and the usual number produce together. [Booking speed](/docs/rules/booking-speed) has the seven levels and where the lines fall.

**A note when a fixed check moved the level.** Sometimes the raw numbers point one way and a fixed check keeps the reading nearer Normal. One of three notes then appears:

| Note | What happened |
|---|---|
| "The raw numbers leaned away from Normal, but the gap was small enough to be ordinary noise at this volume, so we held the call at Normal." | The difference between the count and the usual number was too small to mean anything at that volume. |
| "The raw numbers pointed at an even stronger call, but not by enough evidence to justify it, so we softened it one step." | The count was high, but not by enough bookings to support the strongest level. |
| "We found only a few genuinely comparable nights, so we kept the call within one step of Normal no matter how strong the numbers looked." | Fewer than 5 of the similar nights had any bookings on record, so the reading is capped one step either side of Normal. |

<Example title="The Harbour Inn, a Friday six weeks out">
Friday, November 13, 2026 received 9 bookings in the 30 days to Friday, October 2. The similar Fridays in MAYA's reading of the last three years had about 5 by the same point. The first layer reads:

In the last 30 days, 9 bookings arrived for this night, with 42 days still to go before arrival.
By this point on nights like this one, we would expect about 5 bookings over the same stretch.
That reads as Faster Than Normal.

"Warm-date bump" measures over 30 days and fires at Faster Than Normal or above, so it raised Standard 10%. Blocks for the past week and the past day usually sit under this one too, read for "Hot-week surge" and "Sudden-spike catcher". Each has its own level.
</Example>

### After the night has already been raised or cut

A rule never counts the same bookings twice for the same change. Once a rule has raised a night, it counts only the bookings made since the newest raise still on that night's price by itself or a stronger rule. So does every weaker rule that raises. Cuts work the same way among the rules that cut. A weaker rule's change never moves where a stronger rule counts from, and a raise never moves where a cut counts from, or the other way round. Only booking speed and pickup rules move the count: an occupancy or booking window rule's change never does. The first sentence then names the stretch:

"The newest raise still on this night's price, by the rule behind this reading or a stronger rule, was made on Fri, Oct 2 2026. In the 4 days from that raise on, 3 bookings arrived for it, with 39 days still to go before arrival."

When the raise was earlier the same day, the second sentence reads "Later that day," instead: "... was made on Fri, Oct 2 2026. Later that day, 2 bookings arrived for it, with 42 days still to go before arrival." After a cut it reads "The newest cut still on this night's price", then "In the 3 full days after that, up to yesterday," since a rule that cuts counts whole days from the day after its cut.

On the day of a raise only the bookings that arrived after it are counted, so a burst later the same day is not lost. The "usual" sentence usually compares over that same shorter stretch of the similar nights. A rule that raises on a fast pace (at least Faster Than Normal, Much Faster Than Normal or Surging) is the exception: the bookings since the raise alone have to beat what the similar nights get in its whole window, and the sentence reads "we would expect about 5 bookings over a whole week".

The count starts again only when a price actually changes. A change that comes off because of cancellations no longer counts, so the rule goes back to counting from the change before it, or over its whole window. A change the cancellations leave on stays where it was, and counting still runs from when it was made.

A price you type on that night starts the count over: the rule waits its wait from the moment you typed, then reads its whole window again.

### When there was no history to compare with

A night with no usable similar nights and nothing nearby to read from gets no reading at all, and no booking speed rule is allowed to act on it. The first layer then reads:

"We do not have enough history yet to say what would be normal for this night, so no expectation was formed."
"No booking-speed call was made, and rules that watch booking speed were not allowed to act on this night."

You will see this on a change made by some other rule on a night whose booking speed was checked but could not be read.

## The second layer: "Why did you expect that?"

Click <Ui>Why did you expect that?</Ui> and a short list appears under the first layer. Each line is one rule MAYA followed when choosing the similar nights.

For a rule that cuts, the first line is "A rule that cuts counts full days only, up to yesterday, on this night and on the nights it is compared with alike."

When the night had already been raised or cut, the next line says so: "Once a rule or a stronger one has raised this night, it counts only the bookings made after the newest of those raises still on the price, the rest of that day included, and reads the nights it is compared with over the same days. A weaker rule's raise, or any cut, doesn't move where it starts." For a rule that raises on a fast pace the middle reads "and those alone have to beat what the nights it is compared with get in a whole week" instead. There is a matching line for cuts.

Then, for an ordinary comparison:

- **The weekday.** "We only compared against other Friday nights", followed by the reason: booking rhythms differ too much between days of the week to mix them. Weekends are Friday and Saturday nights. The line also appears on a night near a holiday, where it is not always accurate: around a fixed-date holiday such as Christmas or July 4, the compared nights are the same dates in earlier years and usually fall on other weekdays. The dates under <Ui>Show me the nights you compared</Ui> show which nights were used.
- **The holiday or the season.** A night near a US holiday reads "This night sits in the orbit of Thanksgiving, so we matched against the same position relative to that holiday in earlier years, not the same calendar date." Any other night reads "We stayed inside the same demand season your history shows: Peak Season (June 10 through August 31)." Seasons are stretches of your year that book differently, read from your own history, and this line is the only place they appear on screen. [Which past nights your rules compare with](/docs/rules/similar-nights) has more.
- **When the match was loosened.** "Strict matching found too few usable nights, so we relaxed the criteria a step", with a note that this is worth weighing. It means fewer than 4 nights matched on the strict rule (same weekday, same season), so MAYA allowed the same kind of day (a Saturday for a Friday, since both are weekend nights) or a nearby time of year.
- **Always last.** "Closed periods and any dates you have told us were not normal are left out of the comparison entirely."

### When the reading came from momentum

A night with no similar nights at all is read from momentum instead: how nights near it are filling now, against how the same nights were filling a year ago. The list then opens with two lines that say exactly that, and adds:

- "6 nearby nights could be paired with their counterparts from last year." (or "No nearby night could be paired with a counterpart from last year, so we cannot say whether the pace has changed.")
- "Those nights are filling about 20% faster than they were a year ago." (or "slower", or "at about the same pace as a year ago" when within 5% either way)
- "The starting point was about 4 bookings, taken from how this same night was booking at this point last year (Fri, Nov 14 2025)." (or "taken from the typical pace of nearby nights" when the night itself has no usable history from last year)
- "A momentum read is fuzzier than a direct comparison", with a note to treat it as a best effort rather than a measurement.

[Which past nights your rules compare with](/docs/rules/similar-nights) describes momentum in full.

## The third layer: "Show me the nights you compared"

Once the second layer is open, and when there are nights to show, <Ui>Show me the nights you compared</Ui> appears. Click it for one row per night. The nights nearest in time come first, up to 8. For an ordinary night that is usually the same weekday, week after week, from the same season: first any nights of that season that have already passed this year, then the same season last year, then earlier years if there is room.

Three rows from the list for The Harbour Inn's Friday, November 13, 2026:

| Row | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Fri, Nov 14 2025" then "4 bookings in the same stretch" | That Friday had received 4 bookings over the same stretch, at the same distance before arrival. |
| "Fri, Nov 7 2025" then "1 booking in the same stretch" | The Friday a week before, read the same way. |
| "Fri, Oct 24 2025" then "6 bookings in the same stretch" | The next Friday back. Fri, Oct 31 2025 is not in the list: it is Halloween, and a night near a holiday is never compared with an ordinary night. |

A row can also read "no history for this night". MAYA has no bookings recorded for that night, usually because it falls before your history begins. It adds nothing to the usual number and does not count toward the 5 similar nights a full-strength reading needs.

Under each date, in smaller type, the reasons it was chosen, joined by semicolons: "a Friday; your Shoulder Season; about a year earlier". The season is your own, so its name on your screen can differ. When the match was loosened, the stricter matches are listed first, and a looser row can read "a Saturday, similar to a Friday; your Shoulder Season; about a year earlier" or "a Friday; a nearby time of year; 2 years earlier". The last reason says how long ago the night was: "earlier this year", "recently", "about a year earlier", "2 years earlier" or "3 years earlier".

A row for a night near a holiday reads like "Thanksgiving 2025; 2 days after the holiday; similar weekend placement". The last part appears when the holiday fell on a similar day of the week that year.

For a momentum reading the rows are the paired nights, listed on their year-ago date: "3 bookings at this point last year, against 5 bookings now for Fri, Nov 6 2026", with the reason "used to read how the pace has changed since last year". The starting point night reads "4 bookings at this point last year", marked as the starting point for the estimate, with the reason "this same night a year ago".

On the right of each row is <Ui>Not a fair comparison?</Ui>. Once you have set a night aside, that spot reads "Set aside" with a note that it is not used from the next run onward. [Setting a date aside](/docs/watch/not-a-fair-comparison) walks through the form.

<Example title="Reading a surprise raise">
Sam sees Deluxe raised 25% on Saturday, November 14, 2026 and opens <Ui>How did we know?</Ui>. In the block for the past week, the first layer says 10 bookings arrived in the last 7 days against about 5 usual: Much Faster Than Normal. That is the reading "Hot-week surge" acted on. The second layer says MAYA compared only Saturdays in the same season and left closed periods out.

In this example The Harbour Inn's booking history only goes back to September 2025. The third layer lists 8 Saturdays, nearest first. Five have bookings on record, with 1, 4, 5, 5 and 6 bookings in the same stretch. The three oldest fall before the history begins and read "no history for this night", so they add nothing. The usual number is the average of the middle three of the five, about 5, because with five nights the highest and lowest are left off.

The 1-booking night is Sat, Nov 15 2025. Sam recalls the road closure that weekend and clicks <Ui>Not a fair comparison?</Ui> on it. From the next run that Saturday is left out, and an older Saturday moves up into the list. It too falls before the history begins, so it adds nothing. The usual number stays about 5: the 1-booking night was already left off as the lowest, and the four that remain average 5. But with only four similar Saturdays that have bookings, the reading can now go no further than Faster Than Normal, so "Hot-week surge" (which waits for Much Faster Than Normal) will not fire on this night while that holds. The 25% raise already on the night stays. With a longer history, the Saturday that moved up would have had bookings, and nothing would be capped.
</Example>

## How long the evidence is kept

MAYA keeps the detail behind a change for 90 days. Within that time the numbers open for any change still on the change log. Since the log shows the 10 most recent runs that changed a price, in practice you will nearly always be inside the 90 days.

<Callout kind="not-yet">
There is no page that lists your seasons, your similar nights or your holidays on their own. They appear only inside this panel, for the night in question. You cannot type an expected number or change the level by hand. The one lever is setting a night aside.
</Callout>
