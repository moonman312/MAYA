<InPlainWords>
The <Ui>PMS</Ui> tab lists your room types as your property system reports them, with their unit counts. Untick <Ui>Counts as a room</Ui> for anything nobody sleeps in, and MAYA leaves it out of occupancy and your bill. It is still priced, and sent when you are live, like any other room type. Add <Ui>Rooms out of service</Ui> for units off sale for a stretch, so occupancy reads against what you can really sell.
</InPlainWords>

## Where it is

Open the <Ui>PMS</Ui> tab. Under the connection cards sits a section headed <Ui>Room types</Ui>, with a <Ui>?</Ui> beside the heading. Everyone on the property can see it. Changing anything here needs Revenue Manager access or higher.

## The list

One row per active room type, in name order. Each row shows the room type's name, its unit count ("12 units"), a small tag when it is unticked, and the checkbox <Ui>Counts as a room</Ui>. The tag reads "not a room" when the room type has been unticked, and "needs your answer" when nobody has ticked or unticked it yet. A room type that is not ticked is dimmed.

While the list loads you see a grey placeholder. If it cannot load: "Couldn't load room types." with <Ui>Try again</Ui>. Before the first sync: "No room types have synced yet."

## Counts as a room

A ticked room type is one guests sleep in. Ticked room types are what MAYA divides by. They make up:

- the sellable count behind sellable occupancy, on the day card and in every occupancy rule,
- the room count behind the calendar's colours (revenue per room),
- the room count you are billed for.

A room type tagged "needs your answer" is one nobody has ticked or unticked yet, whose name reads like something nobody sleeps in (see "How MAYA proposes the answer" below). It shows unticked and is left out of your bill until you answer. Until then it still counts in occupancy and in the calendar's revenue per room. Tick it if guests sleep there and it joins your bill. If they do not, tick it and untick it again: it then shows "not a room" and leaves occupancy and the calendar's count too. The <Ui>?</Ui> beside the heading says the same in one line.

Untick a room type and it leaves all three. Its bookings also leave the history booking speed reads, so a court selling six slots a night cannot read as the hotel filling up. It is still priced, and sent when you are live, like any other room type, and its revenue still counts in the day card's revenue figure.

A rule cannot measure an unticked room type. A rule that measures only unticked types measures nothing and never fires. When an occupancy rule measured a mix, the change log says which were left out: "That 82% leaves out Meeting Room." [Measure one set of room types, change another](/docs/rules/measure-one-set-change-another) has the detail.

Tick or untick and the change saves at once. If it does not save you see "That didn't save" with a note to try again, and the box goes back to where it was. Your rules then run again across the 396-night window, since every occupancy figure has moved: within a few minutes for the coming nights, and over the next few cycles for the furthest, nearest nights first. Each change is recorded with who made it.

<Example title="The Harbour Inn's meeting room">
Cloudbeds lists four room types at The Harbour Inn: Standard (12), Deluxe (6), Suite (2) and Meeting Room (1). With Meeting Room unticked, the property has 20 rooms, not 21. A night with 12 rooms booked reads "12/20 rooms" and 60%. If the meeting room is booked that night, its revenue is in the revenue figure, and the percentage stays 60%. Sam is billed for 20 rooms.
</Example>

## How MAYA proposes the answer

When your history is first imported, MAYA reads each room type's name. A name that is certainly not a bedroom (parking, a boardroom, a pickleball court) is proposed as not a room and shown unticked on the review screen, where one click puts it right. A name that only might be (a "Pool Cabana", a "Spa Deluxe") waits for your answer: the review screen shows it unticked with a "needs your answer" tag, and asks "Is "Pool Cabana" actually a room?".

After that, MAYA never unticks a room type on its own. A new room type from your system whose name reads like a bedroom arrives ticked as a room. If its name reads like something nobody sleeps in, it waits for your answer: it shows unticked, tagged "needs your answer", and counts in occupancy (your existing rules do not include it, see "What a new room type starts with" below), but it is left out of your bill until you answer. To answer, tick it if it is a room, or, if it is not, tick it and untick it again. A sync never overwrites an answer you have given. You can change any room type from the <Ui>PMS</Ui> tab at any time.

## Rooms out of service

Under each room type, click <Ui>+ Rooms out of service</Ui>. A short form opens:

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| <Ui>From</Ui> | The first night the rooms are off sale. Required. |
| <Ui>To</Ui> | The last night. Leave it empty for one night. |
| <Ui>Units</Ui> | How many rooms, from 1 up to the room type's unit count. |
| <Ui>Reason (optional)</Ui> | A few words, up to 200 characters. "Renovation" is the hint. |

Click <Ui>Save</Ui> (it reads <Ui>Saving…</Ui> while it works) or <Ui>Cancel</Ui>. The block then lists under the room type as "2 out of service", the first and last night, "· Renovation", and <Ui>Clear</Ui>.

For those nights only, the units come off the room type's sellable count, for your rules and on the calendar day card. Occupancy then reads against what you can sell, so an occupancy rule does not stay quiet because a renovated wing looks empty. Nothing is sent to your property system. Your rules run again on those nights within a few minutes of saving.

<Example title="Two Standard rooms under renovation">
The Harbour Inn takes 2 Standard rooms off sale from November 10 to November 20, 2026. On those nights the property has 18 sellable rooms, so a night with 11 rooms booked reads "11/18 rooms" and 61% instead of 55%. With 15 rooms booked, "Busy nights" (occupancy Greater than 80) fires at 15 of 18, which is 83%. Without the block, 15 of 20 is 75% and the rule would stay quiet.
</Example>

### Limits and messages

| Rule | What the screen says |
|---|---|
| One block covers at most 366 nights | "One block can cover at most 366 nights." |
| Units are whole numbers from 1 | "Units must be a whole number of at least 1." |
| Not more units than the room type has | "Standard has 12 rooms" followed by a note that you cannot block more than that. |
| Blocks stack night by night, never past the count | "Standard already has 8 rooms out of service on some of those nights" followed by how many you can still block. |
| Reason under 200 characters | "Keep the reason under 200 characters." |
| Real dates, last night not before the first | "End date can't be before the start date." |
| Revenue Manager or higher | "This needs Revenue Manager access or higher on this property." |
| 30 changes per person per 10 minutes, shared with typed prices | "That's a lot of changes at once. Give it a minute and try again." |

### Clearing a block

Click <Ui>Clear</Ui> beside the block. The rooms are back on sale from the next run, within a few minutes. MAYA keeps a record of the block and who cleared it, but it no longer shows. A block already cleared by someone else answers "That block is already cleared."

There is no edit. To change a block's dates or units, clear it and add a new one.

### Booking speed is not changed by a block

A block changes occupancy only. Booking speed counts the bookings a night has received against what similar past nights had, and a room off sale changes neither count. [Booking speed](/docs/rules/booking-speed) explains the count.

## Blocks in your property system are not read

MAYA reads bookings and rates from your system, not room blocks, out-of-order rooms or allotments. A wing you close in Cloudbeds, ThinkReservations or Mews still counts as sellable in MAYA until you enter it here. This is also why MAYA's occupancy can differ from your system's headline figure. [Occupancy looks wrong](/docs/wrong/occupancy-looks-wrong) walks through the differences.

## What a new room type starts with

Delete a room type in your system and create it again, and your system treats it as a new room type. So does MAYA, even when the name is the same. Nothing carries over from the old one. The old one stays in MAYA as the tabs above describe, so for a while you may see two room types with the same name. [Email us](mailto:info@modern-hospitality-solutions.com?subject=Switch%20off%20a%20room%20type) and we switch the old one off.

A brand new room type, or a rebuilt one, starts like this:

- **Your rules do not cover it.** A rule covers the room types that were selected when you saved it. A room type added later is not in any of your rules, not even one that covered every room type you had. That goes for rules you built and for the starter rules. The <Ui>Room Types</Ui> column on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab shows what each rule changes.
- **It has no history.** The old type's past stays remain under the old type, so the new one starts with none. A booking speed rule that measures only the new type has nothing to compare with, so it stays quiet at first. Once the type has bookings of its own, readings with fewer than 5 similar nights are capped at one step.
- **Occupancy works at once.** An occupancy rule that measures it reads its bookings against its units from the first cycle. A pickup count rule that measures it waits until MAYA has kept a record of the type for the whole lookback (1, 3 or 7 days).
- **Its floor and ceiling are MAYA's defaults**, $1.00 and $99,999.99, which stop nothing in most currencies (where a room sells above 99,999.99, the default ceiling holds its price down). They stay there until real ones are set for it. A suggestion card is the quickest way (below).
- **Prices you typed stay with the old type.** A manual price belongs to one room type, so the new type starts without any.
- **On Mews, it gets the same unit count as your other room categories**, as the Mews tab above explains. On Cloudbeds and ThinkReservations its own count is read.

To bring the new type into your pricing, add a rule for it, or change an existing rule to include it. A saved rule cannot take on a room type, so changing one means deleting it and adding it again with the new type selected. [Change a rule](/docs/recipes/change-a-rule) walks through it.

Then, on the <Ui>Rules</Ui> tab, click <Ui>Get suggestions from my data</Ui>. The review can offer a floor card and a ceiling card for any room type still at a default. For a type with no history yet, the cards offer your answers to the five questions, if you gave them. Without those, a floor card needs at least 30 room nights sold in that room type, and a ceiling card needs at least 200. A room night is one room for one night at a rate above 0, so a room type with 12 rooms can reach 30 in three full nights. [Floors and ceilings](/docs/review/floors-and-ceilings) has the detail.
